## 61:1

> All that is in the heavens and all that is on earth glorifies Allah, for He is the All-Mighty, the All-Wise.

Surah As-Saff belongs to a sequence concerned with building a believing community after sacrifice has been made, so that what victory has achieved is not lost. Earlier comes Al-Hadid, with its emphasis on striving and spending; the twenty-eighth part of the Quran then addresses the community’s relationships and organization. Here the image is that of a united rank, followed in Al-Jumu‘ah by the gathering, and in Al-Munafiqun by attempts to break that gathering apart. 

Abdullah ibn Salam, may Allah be pleased with him, relates that a group of Companions said: “If we knew which deed was most beloved to Allāh then we would do it.” The opening of this surah was then revealed, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recited it to them (Al-Tirmidhi, 3309; authentic). The whole surah can therefore be read as an answer to a question: what action does Allah love when the call to His religion needs support and sacrifice? 

Beginning the answer with glorification can convey several meanings. Allah has no need of anyone’s obedience and suffers no loss from anyone’s turning away: everything in the heavens and earth glorifies Him. He is Almighty and cannot be overcome, and He is Wise, so His commands are never without wisdom. Just as the angels in heaven are ordered in obedience and ranks, believers on earth are called to stand together in obedience, worship and support of Allah’s cause, rather than breaking away and becoming divided. 

Striving and standing in ranks do not give Allah strength. They are a responsibility that tests the servant’s sincerity within a universe already subject to its Lord.

## 61:2

> O you who believe, why do you say what you do not do?

After glorification comes a direct confrontation with the gap between what believers say and what they do. The problem is not wishing to do good. It is allowing that wish to become an imagined picture of religion, then withdrawing when it becomes a duty that demands something of us. A person may wish to strive in Allah’s cause, call others to Him, seek knowledge or memorize the Quran. Yet when the work requires patience and hardship, they say that it is not for them, or that they have not found the tenderness of heart they were seeking. 

The surah overturns a measure that equates worship solely with a pleasant feeling. Honoring parents, struggling against one’s own weaknesses, remaining steadfast at one’s post and giving to support the religion may all involve moments with no immediate sense of achievement, yet they remain acts of servitude to Allah. This meaning has been linked to those who wished to fight but found it burdensome when it was prescribed: “However, when fighting was ordained for them, a group of them feared people as one should fear Allah or even more.” It also recalls those who preferred the unarmed party in Al-Anfal 8:7. 

The danger goes beyond abandoning a single action. A person may select from the Quran only what stirs their emotions, then turn away from the verses that demand belief, responsibility or sustained effort. Revelation was not sent to provide moments of spiritual feeling detached from action, but to form someone who acts upon what they say.

## 61:3

> It is most loathsome to Allah that you say what you do not do.

The verse does not treat the gulf between words and deeds as a minor lapse: it is deeply hateful to Allah. This does not mean that someone who has sinned must stop enjoining good until they become perfect. The danger is settling into a state in which one no longer cares about reforming oneself, telling others to do what one has stopped striving to do, or making religious speech a substitute for purification and action. 

One way to understand the severity of this condemnation is that someone who speaks in the name of religion and then contradicts it may cause people to detest the religion itself and imagine that the truth cannot be lived. The bearer of the message then becomes a cause of people’s being put off by it. This contradiction also undermines the united rank. As parts of one’s responsibilities are forgotten and knowledge fails to become action, room for argument and hostility grows. 

The Quran describes people who forgot a portion of what they had been reminded of: “So We induced hostility and hatred among them”. Unity is not built by multiplying slogans, but by putting knowledge into practice and continuing to work on one’s own reform while calling others to Allah.

## 61:4

> Indeed, Allah loves those who fight in His way in firm ranks, as if they were a solid structure.

Here comes the answer about the actions Allah loves: fighting in His cause in a cohesive rank. The verse names not only the action but also how it is undertaken. If believers cannot stand together when they are vulnerable and facing battle, when mutual support is most needed, when will they unite? A firmly joined building is more than stones placed side by side. Its stones support one another and are bound together by something that makes them one structure. 

This conveys the brotherhood and relationships through which different roles become a source of strength rather than fragmentation. No individual or group should therefore imagine that they are the entire building. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, compared the prophetic missions to a building with one brick left to be placed, and described himself as that brick (Al-Bukhari, 3535; Muslim, 2286). Someone who knows they are one brick recognizes their need for the rest of the building. 

Their worth lies not in occupying its most visible position, but in remaining firm at the post where the community needs them. A role may seem small to people yet be valued by Allah. When the person who used to clean the mosque—a woman or a man in the narration—died, the Prophet noticed their absence, asked about them and then prayed at their grave (Al-Bukhari, 458; Muslim, 956). A person serving the religion should not despise a useful, enduring role or abandon their post whenever it attracts little interest, leaving others with additional burdens. 

Islamic worship repeatedly trains Muslims to come together: the prayer rows, Friday prayer, Hajj, Ramadan and mutual support through zakah. Their bonds are not merely decorative. Even the prayer row is protected by a specific warning: “If anyone joins up a row, Allah will join him up, but if anyone breaks a row, Allah will cut him off.” (Abu Dawud, 666; Al-Nasa’i, 819; authentic). This also offers a wider lesson in how believers are formed: a Muslim builds relationships between people serving in different roles and seeks what completes the structure, rather than trying to prove that their own brick is the whole building.

## 61:5

> And [remember] when Moses said to his people, “O my people, why do you hurt me when you know that I am a messenger of Allah to you?” So when they chose to deviate, Allah caused their hearts to deviate, for Allah does not guide the rebellious people.

After the image of a firmly joined building, the surah shows what can destroy the rank from within: Moses, peace be upon him, is harmed by people who know that he is Allah’s messenger to them. Among the deepest injuries to a leader on the path of truth is for followers to recognize his sincerity, then leave him without help when duty calls. This is connected to the Children of Israel’s response when commanded to enter the Holy Land. 

They told Moses: “Go, you and your Lord, and fight; we are staying right here!” He eventually said: “O my Lord, I have no control over anyone except myself and my brother”. The verse then states a fearsome consequence: their own deviation came first, followed by Allah’s causing their hearts to deviate. They initially inclined towards worldly desires and chose them; part of their punishment was that their hearts grew further from wanting the truth. 

The need to purify the rank is also illustrated by the hadith about a prophet setting out to fight. He did not want to be followed by someone whose heart was occupied with a marriage not yet consummated, an unfinished house or livestock whose offspring they were awaiting (Al-Bukhari, 3124; Muslim, 1747). A heart that has not settled its direction may become a point of weakness when the confrontation intensifies. A united rank needs not only fewer enemies but hearts less distracted by other concerns. Conflicting directions within people’s hearts prevent a single rank from forming, even when their bodies stand close together.

## 61:6

> And [remember] when Jesus, son of Mary, said, “O Children of Israel, I am truly a messenger of Allah to you, confirming the Torah which came before me, and giving glad tidings of a messenger after me whose name will be Ahmad[1].” But when he came to them with clear proofs, they said, “This is clear magic.”

Against the example of breaking the rank stands Jesus son of Mary, peace be upon him, joining it together. He confirms the Torah before him and announces a messenger to come after him, named Ahmad. He presents himself neither as a beginning with nothing before it nor as an ending with nothing after it. He maintains the connection with the earlier message and makes way for the messenger who will follow. Such conduct builds unity: do not destroy what came before merely because you arrived later, or try to control what comes next because you want all the work to revolve around you. 

[One explanation of the Arabic name «أحمد», Ahmad, connects it with abundant praise and great distinction in that regard. On this reading, Jesus, peace be upon him, announces someone of greater standing than himself in this respect, showing the prophets’ humility and their willingness to give another his rightful place.] A related example is Moses’ acknowledgment of his brother, peace be upon them both: “My brother Aaron is more eloquent than me in speech”. 

Someone who wants the rank to be complete looks for a person who complements them, even if that person excels them in a particular respect. [Two interpretations have been given for the pronoun in «فلما جاءهم بالبينات», concerning the one who came with clear proofs: Jesus, or the messenger whose coming he announced. Both possibilities accord with the unity of their call.] The clear proofs come from the same path, but someone determined to reject them gives them a false label, calling them obvious magic. Even that accusation implicitly acknowledges that an extraordinary effect was seen; the label was changed to avoid accepting what it demonstrated.

## 61:7

> Who could be more wrong than the one who fabricates lies against Allah when invited to Islam? And Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people.

Wrongdoing reaches its height when a person does more than reject guidance and invents a lie about Allah while being invited to submit to Him. The clear proofs have appeared, and the call asks for submission to Allah, yet the person chooses to relabel the truth to make it easier to reject. The Quran is called magic, or the Messenger’s signs are described as something other than what they really are. The problem is not a lack of information, but a heart intent on preserving its position even if that requires giving truth the name of falsehood. 

The description therefore moves from the defiantly disobedient in Moses’ story, where the wrongdoing began with deviation and withholding support, to the wrongdoers here, where rejection has become resistance to the message and fabrication against it. Someone unwilling to change may begin by turning away, then find it necessary to construct an entire account that justifies that refusal.

## 61:8

> They wish to extinguish the light of Allah with their mouths, but Allah will complete His light, even though the disbelievers dislike it.

Rejection does not remain an individual matter; opposition becomes a sustained undertaking aimed at extinguishing Allah’s light. [The Arabic plural present-tense form «يريدون» portrays an intention repeatedly pursued by people acting together.] The aim is not merely to reduce the religion’s influence, but to put out the light that awakens hearts. One interpretation identifies this light as revelation and the Quran, which Allah calls a light: “but We have made it [i.e., the Qur’an] a light by which We guide whom We will of Our slaves.” The image of mouths set against light itself conveys how feeble the means are. 

Attempts to end the religion have been likened to people gathering to blow out the sun. Yet the weakness of those means before Allah’s promise does not diminish the gravity of the wrongdoing. Those who seek to cut people off from the Quran seek to deprive them of a light they need more than food and drink. Against this stands certainty: Allah will complete His light despite the disbelievers’ dislike. The religion does not wait for everyone’s approval in order to endure. A believer’s support of it is an honor for that believer, not the rescue of a religion incapable of surviving.

## 61:9

> It is He Who has sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, so that He may make it prevail over all religions, even though the polytheists may dislike it.

Sending the Messenger is itself part of the promise to complete the light. Allah sent him with guidance and the true religion, which He promised to make prevail over all other religions. The message’s destiny is therefore not measured by a passing period of weakness, or by what any one generation sees within its lifetime. More than one explanation has been given of the fulfillment of this promise. One holds that aspects of it occurred when Islam prevailed and became established; another locates its full realization in the descent of Jesus, peace be upon him. 

The verse need not be confined to a single moment. Just as the preceding verse mentions the disbelievers’ dislike, this verse mentions the dislike of those who associate partners with Allah. Their hostility may remain, but it does not give them control over the outcome. The One Who sent the Messenger is the One Who promised the religion’s ascendancy. The believer works in the light of that promise rather than making the changing circumstances of the moment their measure.

## 61:10

> O you who believe, shall I tell you about a trade that will save you from a painful punishment?

This second address to the believers follows the opening warning against words that never become deeds. They are now offered a trade that saves them from a painful punishment: the question moves from wishing to a practical commitment. Calling it a trade corrects the attitude of someone working for the religion. They are not giving as though doing Allah and His cause a favor, then waiting for people to repay them. They themselves need this trade, because it is they who are saved by it. 

Their chief motive should therefore be neither a position within the rank, personal heroism nor a feeling of being indispensable, but escape from the Fire and a gain whose value does not collapse when this world ends. Whoever understands this becomes less attached to having their name mentioned or seeing the result of their own work. They joined the rank seeking salvation, not an opportunity to prove their own importance.

## 61:11

> It is to believe in Allah and His Messenger and to struggle in the way of Allah with your wealth and your lives. That is better for you, if only you knew.

The trade begins with faith in Allah and His Messenger, because sacrifice unsupported by sound faith does not build the rank intended here. It is followed by striving in Allah’s cause with wealth and life. [The Arabic «الجهاد», striving in Allah’s cause, is broader than «القتال», fighting, mentioned in verse 4. Fighting is one particular form; the broader term also includes giving wealth, expending effort and enduring hardship to support the truth.] At the surah’s end, support will be expressed more broadly still, leaving every believer a way to contribute according to their ability. 

Inability to undertake one form does not excuse abandoning them all: a believer gives whatever support they can. Wealth repeatedly comes before life in verses about striving, because wealth has a strong hold on the heart, even though life remains dearer and more precious. The verse then settles the question of gain: such striving is better for the believers themselves, if they understand. What a believer gives is not a loss imposed upon them; seen in the full reckoning, it is their own good. The rank does not wait for someone else to do everything. Each brick turns its faith into a real contribution.

## 61:12

> He will forgive you your sins and admit you to gardens under which rivers flow, and pleasant dwellings in Gardens of Eternity. That is the supreme triumph.

Even after faith and striving, the Quran does not portray the believer who serves as someone free of shortcomings. The first reward mentioned is forgiveness of sins. While giving, that person still needs Allah’s forgiveness; their work does not make them a benefactor to whom Allah owes a favor. Then come the gardens and their good dwellings. There is a beautiful correspondence here: someone who leaves the settled comfort of this world to support the religion does not lose what they leave behind. 

A better dwelling awaits with Allah. [The Arabic «عدن» carries the sense of abiding and remaining settled.] Great success is thus defined as forgiveness and Paradise before worldly victory is mentioned. A person may fight and be killed, or give a lifetime without witnessing the victory. If they attain this outcome, they have not missed success. This understanding keeps the rank steadfast even when no earthly fruit appears quickly.

## 61:13

> And [He will grant you] another favor that you love: help from Allah [against your enemies] and an imminent conquest. Give glad tidings to the believers.

After placing the greater reward in the Hereafter first, the Quran does not overlook the human desire to see a more immediate result. Help from Allah and a near victory are also promised as glad tidings in this world. One interpretation identifies the near victory as the conquest of Makkah, which did take place. But it is the order of the two promises that forms the heart: forgiveness and Paradise are the great success; then comes the victory people love, to strengthen them and help them along the way. Allah knows that people want signs through which they can see the fruit of their sacrifice, and He gives them this good news. Yet He does not make continued obedience conditional on each person witnessing the victory. A believer acts because they are trading with Allah, then welcomes whatever openings Allah grants in this world.

## 61:14

> O you who believe, be supporters of Allah’s cause, as Jesus, son of Mary, said to the disciples, “Who will be my supporters for Allah’s cause?” The disciples said, “We are the supporters of Allah’s cause.” Then a group of the Children of Israel believed and another disbelieved. We then supported those who believed against their enemies, so they prevailed.

The third address in the surah asks for something deeper than a passing action: believers are to be supporters of Allah’s cause. [The Arabic «كونوا أنصار الله» asks them to be supporters, rather than merely telling them to offer support on one occasion. Support is to become an enduring quality of their lives.] The path that began with fighting and then broadened to striving now widens further. Those able to fight do so; those unable to do so contribute whatever striving and support they can. 

Those living under such weakness that they have no strength to wield remain steadfast upon the truth without changing their commitment. The disciples provide an example: when Jesus, peace be upon him, asked who would support him in Allah’s cause, they declared themselves supporters of Allah. [The Arabic word «الحواري», used of a disciple, has been explained in terms of purity and sincerity.] Religious work is not sustained by a crowd enthusiastic for an hour that disperses when sacrifice is required. 

It needs a firm, sincere core that carries the work when others weaken. Several interpretations have been given of the believers’ being supported against their enemy and becoming ascendant. One is that they prevailed through strength and victory, with part of its fulfillment coming through the mission of Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, and the clarification of the truth about Jesus. Another is that, during periods of weakness, believers retained the upper hand through sound proof and steadfastness even when physically overcome. 

The shared lesson is that changing circumstances do not end the duty to support the truth. If strength cannot bring you the upper hand, hold firmly to the truth and do not abandon it. The end of the surah thus returns to its opening question. The action most beloved to Allah is not merely a wish expressed aloud, but faith that becomes a united rank, steadfast service at one’s post and sacrifice, and a life shaped by the choice to support Allah’s cause.
