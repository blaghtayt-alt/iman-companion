## Introduction

It is part of Allah’s mercy that He has not left His servants without purpose. He sent messengers and revealed scriptures so that people would have clear guidance, as He says: «so that those who chose to perish would perish after seeing clear evidence, and those who chose to survive would survive after seeing clear evidence». Among the greatest expressions of that mercy is the preserved Quran, through which a person receives their Lord’s guidance and learns how to worship Him. Surah Ad-Duha shows the importance of this blessing in the Prophet’s life and reassures him when revelation was delayed. His opponents’ words were not to determine how he understood what was happening.

Gratitude for the Quran means holding fast to it, not merely having a copy at home. The Prophet ﷺ described what he had left among his community: «if you hold on to it, you will never again go astray, that is Allah’s Book». The effort this requires appears in Allah’s command: «So hold fast to what is revealed to you». A servant strives against whatever distracts them from reading, reflecting and acting, and does not inwardly resist revelation. They then convey its guidance to others, for the message was not revealed to a closed circle: «Blessed is the One Who has sent down the Criterion to His slave, so that he may be a warner to the worlds».

Ad-Duha honors and defends the Prophet ﷺ, as do Ash-Sharh and Al-Kawthar. Harming him and speaking maliciously against him are serious matters; revelation answered those who claimed that his Lord had abandoned him. Loving him includes recognizing his worth, defending him truthfully and following his guidance. The Surah moves from reassuring his heart and opening the prospect of hope to recalling earlier care, then to gratitude expressed in mercy toward the vulnerable, teaching those in need and acknowledging Allah’s blessings.

## 93:1

> By the morning sunlight,

«By the morning sunlight» is an oath by the time when light spreads and activity begins; the oath by night follows. This scene, joined to the answer to the oath, brings reassurance: the One who brings morning light after darkness has not abandoned His Prophet when revelation is delayed. The point is more than enjoying a beautiful morning. The heart receives this light while hearing that it has been neither forsaken nor hated. A delay in something one loves is not, by itself, evidence that Allah’s care has ceased.

An authentic account of the revelation relates that Gabriel was slow to come to the Prophet ﷺ. The polytheists claimed that Muhammad had been abandoned, and the verses were revealed. Another authentic report relates that he ﷺ was ill and did not stand in prayer for two or three nights. A woman came to gloat, claiming that his devil had left him, and Allah revealed the beginning of the Surah. Both reports concern its revelation, but neither explicitly states that the delay in revelation caused the physical illness. Reflecting on how deeply he longed for revelation is different from asserting what caused his illness.

There had also been a pause in revelation early in the mission, after it first began, followed by the command to warn in Surah Al-Muddaththir. The delay associated with Ad-Duha was distinct from that initial pause. The reports cited about this Surah’s revelation do not specify how long its delay lasted. What matters here is that the polytheists exploited the delay to attack him, treating the interruption of something they had denied as evidence against its recipient. Yet both the coming of revelation and its delay were by Allah’s command.

The mention of two or three nights reveals the place of night prayer in the Prophet’s life. The report counts the nights he missed because of illness, whereas some of us may remember only a few nights on which we prayed. This does not blame a sick person who has an excuse. It awakens a sense of what a regular relationship with the Quran means: having it as nourishment for the heart, feeling the value of what one misses, rather than treating it as an optional extra whose presence and absence make no difference.

Ibn Abbas’s report gives a clear example of the Quran’s effect on conduct. The Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and his generosity was especially great in Ramadan, when Gabriel met him to study the Quran. They studied it every night of Ramadan. Ibn Abbas described him as «more generous than the fast wind». Studying revelation was not simply gathering information; it was accompanied by greater generosity and giving. Benefit begins with being changed by the Quran, and its effects then reach others. Recitation is thus connected to mercy and generosity.

One illustration of this longing is a person who tastes the sweetness of the Quran, night prayer, supplication and tears during Ramadan, then becomes so occupied with Eid and the days that follow that they drift away. They may feel an emptiness that entertainment and abundant company cannot fill, because they have lost what brought comfort to their heart. One reflection on times of waiting is that they allow people to revisit, repeat and use what they have learned, rather than always seeking something new from revelation. This is an educational reflection, not a definite identification of every wisdom behind a delay in revelation.

A comparable oath affirming the Prophet’s guidance is: «By the star when it sets» and «Your companion [i.e., the Prophet] has neither strayed nor is he deluded». A star’s movement and disappearance, and the alternation of light and darkness, do not provide grounds for imagining that revelation’s guidance has gone out. This is a reflective comparison between images of light and the guidance of revelation. Guidance is not measured by the disappearance that affects celestial bodies.

## 93:2

> and by the night when it covers with darkness[1],

«and by the night when it covers with darkness» evokes night as it becomes still and darkness spreads. To someone whose wait has grown long, it can seem motionless. [The Arabic «سَجَىٰ» evokes this stillness and covering. The related expression «مُسَجّى» is used of a covered body.] A person undergoing a severe trial may feel that a night lasts a year, not because time has stopped, but because pain makes its passing heavy. From this perspective, one may reflect on the contrast between the night’s stillness and the spread of morning light, without letting a prolonged experience of darkness determine one’s judgment of the whole future.

Night itself is not useless time. It brings stillness and rest, just as the morning brings movement and work. Another reflection is that daytime offers opportunities to serve the religion and benefit people, while night offers solitude with Allah and prayer before Him. A believer needs work and rest, service to others and renewal of their relationship with their Lord. One need must not cancel out the other: rest should not become abandonment of obedience, nor activity neglect of the heart’s nourishment.

Every condition calls for an appropriate form of worship: gratitude in well-being, patience in trial, giving when one receives, and refuge in Allah and steadfastness when one is denied. A person does not wait for painless circumstances before worshiping Allah, but asks what obedience is possible in the circumstances they have. Considering the alternation of night and morning thus extends beyond dividing up the hours. It invites us to consider changing conditions while maintaining our connection with our Lord through them all.

Human beings need companionship. Adam and Eve offer one way of reflecting on this need, as does the description of the people of Paradise «seated on couches facing one another». A person may face circumstances in which nobody is present to accompany them or listen, yet they can still find comfort in their Lord’s words. The need for company is not a fault. Its absence, however, does not mean that the heart has been left without anything to steady it. An ayah may bring consolation that helps someone bear what they are experiencing.

A story is told of a Palestinian prisoner who was asked, after his release, how he and his companions had endured imprisonment and torture. He described remembering Allah, reading the Quran and drawing strength from the patience of the prophets, including those of firm resolve. This is an illustrative account, not a Prophetic report. Its point is that a reader remembers those who were patient before them and realizes that they are not alone in being tested, and that Allah’s mercy did not cease to reach His servants through their hardships.

The Quran itself explains this purpose. When those who denied it objected to its gradual revelation, the answer included: «so that We may strengthen your heart thereby». Allah also says: «We relate to you [O Prophet] the stories of the messengers to strengthen your heart». Recitation during hardship is not escape from reality; it is receiving what strengthens the heart to remain steadfast within it. A person reads about what the messengers endured and the care they received, learning patience and striving to remain patient rather than confronting pain apart from revelation.

## 93:3

> your Lord has neither forsaken you [O Prophet] nor hated you;

«your Lord has neither forsaken you [O Prophet] nor hated you» answers the oath: his Lord has neither left him nor hated him. Denying abandonment is different from denying hatred. In human relationships, someone may be forced to leave a loved one, or stay with a person they dislike. Both possibilities are denied here, removing the fear of separation and the fear of hatred together. Revelation’s delay is not a farewell, and the mockery of the polytheists tells us nothing about the Prophet’s standing with his Lord.

There is tenderness in the description ‘your Lord’: the One who cares for and nurtures you is your Lord. The delay of a particular gift does not place you outside His care. [In the Arabic «وَمَا قَلَىٰ», ‘nor hated,’ the object pronoun meaning ‘you’ is not repeated, although the intended object is clear. One reflection is that the word for hatred and the pronoun referring to the Prophet ﷺ are not joined in the wording. This is a reflective observation about the expression’s tenderness, not a claim that it is the only explanation for omitting the object.]

As trials follow one another, a person’s need for reassurance can become intense. Is what I face a sign that I have been abandoned, or a trial through which I should be patient? A supplication reported after Taif illustrates concern for Allah’s pleasure: «If You are not angry with me, then I care not». Al-Albani judged its chain weak, so it must not be attributed to the Prophet ﷺ with certainty. The meaning illustrated here is that Allah’s pleasure matters more to His servant than having every event take the form they desire.

It is authentically reported that the Prophet ﷺ asked Gabriel: «What makes you not visit us more than you do?» Allah then revealed: «We do not descend except with the command of your Lord». The angel is a servant under command and does not descend merely by his own choice. The verse ends: «Your Lord is never forgetful». Delay therefore does not mean forgetfulness. A person may not see the wisdom in what is being arranged, but they themselves are not absent from their Lord’s knowledge and care.

This meaning can be seen in the circumstances of the prophets: Abraham in the fire, Muhammad ﷺ in the cave, Joseph in prison, Jonah in the belly of the fish, and Job in his trial. Neither the harshness of their surroundings nor the length of their pain meant that Allah had abandoned them. A believer learns from their accounts not to use difficult circumstances alone to judge their relationship with their Lord. Trial and care can coexist, even when one cannot see every aspect of His kindness at the time.

The question then is what a servant should do while waiting: «Lord of the heavens and earth and all that is between them. So worship Him and be constant in worshiping Him. Do you know anyone equal to Him?» Do not suspend worship while waiting for what burdens you to end. Strive to continue as far as you are able. [One explanation of the Arabic «سَمِيًّا» is an equal or counterpart.] None is equal to Allah in His lordship, mercy or provision. If you have no Lord besides Him, to whom would you turn away when life becomes difficult?

A believer may receive a share of Allah’s protection and support through following the Prophet ﷺ. This does not mean attaining the rank of the prophets or their particular gifts, such as revelation. In the same way, one can reflect on the words addressed specifically to Moses: «I have chosen you for Myself», while preserving the distinction between prophethood and the care hoped for by the prophets’ followers. The connection between Allah’s sufficiency and following His Messenger appears in: «O Prophet, Allah is sufficient for you and for the believers who follow you». On this interpretation, Allah is sufficient for His Prophet and for his believing followers.

Ibn Taymiyyah expressed this relationship by explaining that complete sufficiency accompanies complete following, while a lesser degree accompanies a lesser degree of following. Seeking a share in these glad tidings calls for following the Prophet, not wishing for a rank without working toward it. Nor is this a promise that every follower will be spared pain. The prophets, who followed Allah’s command most completely, were themselves tested. The believer hopes for Allah’s kindness and sufficient care while remaining patient and obedient, without inventing a deadline for relief or insisting on the form it must take.

## 93:4

> the Hereafter is better for you than the present life;

«the Hereafter is better for you than the present life» moves the heart from the assurance that it has not been abandoned to a promise of good. One of its stated meanings is that the Hereafter is better for the Prophet ﷺ than worldly life. Allah’s gifts are not confined to a short time or a passing painful condition. What He has stored for him is not less than what He has already given, and a delay in some of what he loves is not the end of the favor awaiting him.

It has also been said that the meaning includes the later condition compared with the earlier one. The Prophet’s mission would advance toward greater good, honor and the spread of the message. The period of weakness in Makkah would not be its final scene: migration, Madinah and the growth of the call would follow. This does not make every worldly moment free of difficulty. It prevents the understanding of his life from being confined to one moment of harm or delay.

A believer’s response to this promise includes striving in obedience and following the Prophet, and hoping to grow in their relationship with Allah rather than remaining imprisoned by their present condition. Hope does not mean abandoning action while waiting for tomorrow. It means continuing to call to Allah, pray at night and remain patient. Hope for good to come remains joined to the good of the Hereafter, so that immediate comfort does not become the only measure of Allah’s favor.

## 93:5

> and your Lord will certainly give you so much that you will be well pleased.

«and your Lord will certainly give you so much that you will be well pleased» promises a gift to come. [In the Arabic «وَلَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرْضَىٰ», the lām emphasizes the promise and «سوف» points to the future. The verb «يُعْطِيكَ», ‘give you,’ does not name a particular gift.] One reflection is that the prospect of giving extends beyond a single form: Quran and guidance, victory and conquest, intercession and an honored station, Paradise and good for the community. The outcome is stated as satisfaction, without restricting the gifts that bring it about. The giver is his Lord, who cares for him, not a limited source whose favor ends with the first blessing.

This promise may be considered alongside Moses’s words: «I hastened to You, my Lord, to please You». He hastens in obedience, seeking his Lord’s pleasure; here Allah promises His Prophet ﷺ gifts that will please him. Together, the two meanings show a servant seeking Allah’s pleasure and Allah bestowing honor upon His servant. Satisfaction is not detached from the path of worship: the Prophet ﷺ gives, calls to Allah and stands in prayer, and his Lord promises what will bring him joy.

One lesson from this giving is to learn contentment with Allah’s apportionment, rather than requiring His decree always to match one’s desires. A person may think that their chosen outcome must be best, although they know neither the unseen nor all the consequences. Speaking of the Messenger ﷺ among the believers, Allah says: «If he were to obey you in many matters, you would surely suffer». They would face hardship. The verse concerns following the Messenger’s guidance; its meaning also helps us reflect on why human wishes alone do not determine what is beneficial. In matters of divine decree too, a person trusts their Lord’s wisdom rather than setting their limited understanding above it.

Effort, obedience and supplication therefore continue during trial, as Job prayed: «I have been struck by adversity, and You are the Most Merciful of those who show mercy». Allah then said: «So We responded to him and relieved his adversity, and We restored to him his family, twice as many, as a mercy from Us and as a reminder for Our worshipers». The mention of worshipers makes his story a reminder to those worshiping Allah while in pain. Trial has not shut the door to mercy, and asking for relief does not contradict contentment with Him.

One of the clearest accounts linking the Prophet’s satisfaction to the good of his community concerns his weeping out of compassion for them. He recited Allah’s words in Abraham’s prayer: «My Lord, they have led many people astray. So whoever follows me, he belongs to me», and the words of Jesus, peace be upon him: «If You punish them, they are Your slaves; if You forgive them, You are indeed the All-Mighty, the All-Wise». He then raised his hands, said «O Allah, my Ummah, my Ummah», and wept.

Allah sent Gabriel to ask why he was weeping, although He knew best. He then commanded Gabriel to tell him: «We will please you with regard to your Ummah and will not displease you». The Prophet’s concern was not for his own salvation alone. He cared deeply about those who believed in him, so this glad tiding answered something profound in his heart.

One illustration is a father anxious about his child’s future. When he sees that the child’s life has become upright and settled, he feels reassured and his burden lightens. From this perspective, one may reflect on the Prophet’s joy at the spread of the religion: «When there comes Allah’s help and the conquest» and «and you see people entering Allah’s religion in multitudes». Ibn Abbas understood Surah An-Nasr to announce that the Prophet’s death was approaching, and Umar agreed with him. Thus the end of his life brought both the glad tidings of the message’s spread and the completion of the mission with which he had been sent.

His hope for his community is connected with the intercession and the Reservoir with which he was honored. He said of the Reservoir: «I am your predecessor at the Lake-Fount», meaning that he would reach it ahead of his community. The gifts that please him are not detached from those for whom he desired guidance and salvation. These glad tidings should move a believer to follow him sincerely, rather than rest on wishes or mistake them for a promise of salvation without faith or deeds.

Reassurance thus unfolds over three verses: the denial of abandonment and hatred, the promise of better things, and the promise of gifts that bring satisfaction. Anyone longing for a share in this favor should strive to obey Allah and serve His religion, in times of activity and times of solitude. They trust that their Lord knows best what will benefit them and is too generous for His gifts to be confined to one form a servant proposes.

## 93:6

> Did He not find you an orphan then take good care of you?

«Did He not find you an orphan then take good care of you?» turns the heart, after a promise about the future, toward the care it has known in the past. When a trial involving family, wealth or health lasts, a person may become trapped in the present, forgetting earlier kindness and overlooking good that may still come. This reminder restores what pain has obscured. Allah’s care did not begin today, such that present events would be the only evidence to consider.

An experience close to every human being is their own beginning, before they possessed any means of helping themselves: «He creates you in the wombs of your mothers [in stages], creation after creation, in three layers of darkness». Allah cared for them when they did not know how to ask or protect themselves. They had performed no act of obedience by which they could claim to have earned that blessing. Reflecting on this first kindness prevents a person from limiting their Lord’s power to the means they can arrange for themselves as an adult.

The Prophet ﷺ was an orphan. His father died before his birth; he then lost his mother while young, followed by his grandfather, and entered his uncle’s care. His later life also shows the continuity of Allah’s care. He subsequently lost his uncle and Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, and left the Makkah he loved. These later events did not precede Ad-Duha’s revelation. They are recalled when considering his life as a whole: he lost people he loved, but moving from one condition to another did not mean being cut off from his Lord’s care.

[The Arabic «فَآوَىٰ» speaks of giving shelter, rather than merely feeding him or enriching him. Shelter conveys security and reassurance, beyond meeting hunger.] A problem may distress you while its solution remains out of sight. Remember occasions when Allah cared for you before you could see a way out. Your inability to imagine the means does not mean that the doors to His kindness are closed. Take the steps you can, and keep your heart attached to the One who cared for you when you possessed nothing.

## 93:7

> Did He not find you unguided then guide you?

«Did He not find you unguided then guide you?» is understood here as not knowing the details of revelation before it came, rather than worshiping idols or being misguided in disbelief. The Prophet ﷺ saw the falsehood of idolatry and the distortions affecting religious traditions, and loved solitude and worship. Yet detailed knowledge of Allah’s will and the way to worship Him cannot be obtained simply by rejecting falsehood. Revelation brought what he had not known and made clear the path he would convey and follow.

One of the heaviest burdens a person can bear is longing to reach the truth without knowing the way. Some people who have embraced Islam describe the confusion of searching and moving between understandings in which they found no reassurance, until they came to know Islam. For someone who has experienced that search, guidance is not merely another piece of information. It reveals a direction they lacked and relieves a persistent question: how do I worship my Lord in the way He loves?

One suggested connection with Surah Ash-Sharh is that knowing this path helps reassure the heart and lift the burden of uncertainty. One may therefore reflect on «Have We not reassured your heart for you [O Prophet]» and «and relieved you of your burden» in light of the blessing of explanation and guidance. This is one perspective on the removal of a burden, not an exclusive explanation of the two verses. Its purpose is to appreciate the blessing a seeker receives on learning what pleases their Lord after searching for it.

A person who loves Allah therefore hears «If you love Allah then follow me; Allah will love you» as a practical answer to the love in their heart. They have not been left to invent a path of their own; they have been directed to follow the Prophet ﷺ. Just as the gift of guidance brings them out of uncertainty, gratitude for it means walking the path they have learned. Love for Allah should appear in following His Messenger’s Sunnah, rather than remain a feeling whose practical implications are unknown.

## 93:8

> Did He not find you in need then enrich you?

«Did He not find you in need then enrich you?» recalls relief from need. [The Arabic «عَائِلًا» means poor and in need.] The Prophet’s life combined patience when money was scarce with gratitude and generosity when Allah granted abundance. Some prophets showed patience through severe trial, while others showed gratitude for the dominion and plenty they received. In our Prophet’s life, a servant learns how to worship their Lord both in need and when receiving His gifts.

The forms of richness that comfort the heart are not confined to material things. Good companions who help a person along the path are among Allah’s blessings. In the Prophet’s life, such care included the companionship of Abu Bakr As-Siddiq and, later, the Ansar and Madinah, with the support and assistance he found there. This may also be considered alongside the earlier mention of shelter. These are later examples of continuing care, not a claim that migration to Madinah preceded the Surah’s revelation. A person may have money yet still need a sincere friend, showing that blessing extends beyond a financial account.

Shelter, guidance and relief from need are followed by three responsibilities: mercy toward the orphan, gentleness toward the person asking, and acknowledging blessings. Remembering the past is not simply enjoying a memory; it prepares the heart for gratitude. The care Allah has given you should affect your dealings with others. Someone who remembers their own need before it was met can better understand the person who stands in need today, rather than behave as though they had never needed or received anything.

## 93:9

> So do not mistreat the orphan,

«So do not mistreat the orphan» is the first practical response to the reminder of blessings. The prohibition corresponds to «Did He not find you an orphan then take good care of you?» Just as you received care in your vulnerability, do not make an orphan’s weakness an opportunity for oppression. Do not belittle them for having lost their father, deny their rights, or add heart-breaking humiliation to the pain of loss. Gratitude here moves from acknowledging what one has been given to showing mercy to someone in need.

The shelter and care Allah gives His servant exceed what a created being can provide. This does not cancel a person’s responsibility for what they can do. Someone unable to offer every form of care can still refrain from injustice and do whatever good is within reach. You are not asked to claim the power to remove every pain from an orphan’s life, but to avoid causing their oppression and to use the good you have received to offer them comfort.

This meaning extends to gratitude for other blessings. Someone whom Allah gives wealth can share it with those in need; someone given health can help those unable to do what they can; someone taught knowledge can open a way for others through it. Every blessing offers an appropriate way of benefiting people and should not remain confined to its recipient. Remembering that its source was a favor they did not create for themselves makes it easier to give some of it away and use it to show mercy to the vulnerable.

## 93:10

> nor repulse the beggar[2];

«nor repulse the beggar» includes someone asking for money; someone seeking knowledge and guidance is also mentioned in its interpretation. Each comes with a need, which should not be met with harshness or humiliation. One connection with the preceding verses is that this command corresponds to «Did He not find you unguided then guide you?» After Allah has taught you His path, do not make someone asking about it regret approaching you. What you have learned must not become a barrier preventing someone else from learning.

The Prophet’s welcome to the delegation of Abd Al-Qays offers a clear example. They had come from far away, with the unbelievers of Mudar between their home and him, and could reach him only during a sacred month. He greeted them with «Welcome, O people» and said: «Neither will you have disgrace nor will you regret». They asked for teaching they could convey to those back home and act upon to enter Paradise. He taught them, told them «Memorize them (these instructions)», and instructed them to pass what they learned to those they had left behind.

He began with a welcome that removed feelings of shame and regret, rather than a cold reception that discouraged asking. A warm welcome is thus part of making knowledge accessible. The learner can hear the truth without fearing that their need will be used to belittle them.

The Quran affirms this meaning by instructing the Prophet to say to those who believe in Allah’s verses: «Peace be upon you. Your Lord has decreed mercy upon Himself». It then opens the way back for someone who has done wrong: «whoever among you commits a sin out of ignorance then repents afterwards and mends his ways, then Allah is indeed All-Forgiving, Most Merciful». A person approaching religion needs mercy and peace, and needs to understand that a mistake does not close the door to repentance and reform. Just as Allah has brought you relief through guidance, let the way you teach help others receive it, rather than drive them away.

## 93:11

> and proclaim the favors of your Lord[3].

«and proclaim the favors of your Lord» completes gratitude for relief from need and for every other gift. Acknowledging blessings means recognizing and thanking the Giver, rather than boasting to others about what you possess. Speak of what Allah has given you, do not attribute the favor to yourself alone, and choose people who wish you well with whom to share your joy. [The Arabic «رَبِّكَ», ‘your Lord,’ directs attention back to the source of the gift.] The aim is to honor your Lord’s favor, not magnify yourself.

The Quran is the greatest blessing repeatedly recalled throughout the Surah. Through it came good news, reassurance and guidance. Gratitude includes sharing it, teaching its meanings and acting upon it. Do not speak only of gains in money or comfort while overlooking what taught you about your Lord and how to worship Him. Anyone given the Quran should recognize its value and direct others to the good they have found in it, rather than remain endlessly impressed by what others possess.

The Hadith states: «There should be no envy except in two (cases)». The Prophet ﷺ then described a man whom Allah taught the Quran, who recites it during the night and day, and a man whom Allah gave wealth, who spends it rightly. Others wish to receive similar gifts so that they can do the same. The aspiration is to have comparable good without wishing it to be taken from its recipient. Notice that it concerns benefiting from the gift, not merely possessing it: reciting and acting on the Quran, and spending wealth rightly, rather than simply owning a Quran or having a large balance.

Abdullah ibn Amr is reported to have said, in honoring this blessing, that someone given the Quran who considers another person to have received something better has belittled what Allah magnified and magnified what Allah made small. This is a report from a Companion, not a Hadith attributed to the Prophet ﷺ. Its point here is that a person who carries the Quran should not think little of the guidance they received because someone else has more worldly comfort. Blessings are not measured only by visible possessions.

The opposite of acknowledging blessings is a tongue that continually counts misfortunes and forgets everything else. A person may enjoy many blessings of health, then experience a small ailment and repeat its story in every gathering as though their life had contained nothing else. Reflecting on the well-being that remains reveals much deserving of praise. This does not deny the pain. It prevents pain from erasing every other blessing from memory, or complaint from becoming denial of Allah’s favor.

Jacob, peace be upon him, said: «I only complain of my anguish and sorrow to Allah». Taking one’s pain to Allah is different from objecting to Him and forgetting His kindness. Al-Hasan Al-Basri is reported to have explained «indeed, man is very ungrateful to his Lord» as counting misfortunes and forgetting blessings. Surah Ad-Duha cultivates the opposite habit: remember shelter, guidance and relief from need, then let those memories remain present in your gratitude and actions.

One way to acknowledge this blessing is to learn the Surah and teach it to your family, children and neighbors, and act on what you understand, even if it is one ayah. Describing the Prophet ﷺ as a Quran walking among people expresses the effect of revelation on his character; that phrasing is not a Hadith. What is authentically reported from Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, is: «Indeed, the Prophet's character was the Qur’an». Following him does not end with memorizing words. It becomes visible in gentleness toward someone asking, mercy toward the orphan and gratitude for what one has received.

One contrast remains to awaken the heart. The coming of revelation and its delay lay outside the Prophet’s control, yet he longed to meet Gabriel and receive his Lord’s words. A Quran may be readily at hand for us while we choose to be occupied elsewhere. Gratitude means returning to recitation, reflection, night prayer and action, rather than becoming so accustomed to distance that we no longer feel what we are missing. Speaking of the Quran then grows out of living with it, rather than describing a blessing whose effect cannot be seen in its recipient.
