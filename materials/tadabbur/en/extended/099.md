## Introduction

Allah’s mercy toward His servants includes not leaving them without purpose or guidance. He sent revelation to show them the way to salvation and move them from heedlessness to action. Merely knowing the commands does not give a person all the strength they need to resist their desires and give what they love in obedience to Allah. The soul also needs something to awaken and restrain it, and to remind it of this world’s reality—a reality it may forget amid the demands of life, or deliberately ignore so that it can indulge its wishes.

This is why Surah Al-Hadid first says: Know that the life of this world is no more than play, amusement, adornment, boasting among yourselves, and competition in wealth and children, and then commands: Race one another towards forgiveness from your Lord and Paradise the width of which is like the width of the heaven and earth. Recognizing that worldly enjoyment passes away helps a person hasten toward what lasts. In this same way, Surah Az-Zalzalah awakens the heart with the scene of the familiar earth losing its stability, then brings it back to the measure of deeds. It begins with the earth’s earthquake and ends with an atom’s weight, so that people value what Allah has declared important, rather than judging good and evil by social convention, wealth or personal desire.

## 99:1

> When the earth is shaken with a mighty quake,

The surah opens with a shaking that interrupts the routine of a life people imagine will continue unchanged. A person wakes, goes to work, returns and sleeps, forming wishes and pursuing them until the cycle becomes familiar and they forget that it will end. [The opening Arabic word إِذَا, ‘when,’ places them before an event that will certainly come.] It surprises the heedless even though they have repeatedly been warned of it. This is not an earthquake confined to a region, which people living far away hear about. It is the shaking of the whole earth itself. That is why the human being asks in astonishment: “What is the matter with it?”, despite having known tremors and disasters before.

[In زِلْزَالَهَا, the shaking is attributed to the earth: its own immense earthquake, before which all those previously known to humanity seem small.] A related Arabic construction appears in Allah’s words: But whoever desires the Hereafter and strives for it as he should, being a believer. [The phrase سَعَىٰ لَهَا سَعْيَهَا refers to the striving appropriate to seeking the Hereafter, not just any effort. Likewise, زِلْزَالَهَا points to something far beyond an ordinary, familiar earthquake.] [The form of the Arabic word for shaking also conveys repeated, successive disturbance.] In an ordinary earthquake, a person waits for the tremor to end and hopes to return home, or build another house if theirs has fallen. This scene overturns the expectation that a few moments will pass and life will resume as before.

Allah says: O people, fear your Lord; the earthquake of the Hour is something dreadful. On the Day when you see it, every nursing mother will abandon her baby, and every pregnant woman will miscarry. You will see people as though they are drunk, while they are not drunk, but the punishment of Allah is severe. A nursing mother, with her child as close to her as can be, is distracted from that child—not because she cruelly wishes to cast it aside, but because terror overwhelms her until she loses awareness of what held her attention. Every nursing mother is affected, and people are so overcome that they appear drunk without being intoxicated. The greatness of this earthquake cannot be fully imagined from disasters people have witnessed in this world.

[The Arabic زُلْزِلَتْ is passive: the earth is shaken, without the agent being named in the sentence.] None but Allah can overturn the entire earth in this way. The earth’s stability, on which people base their plans and hopes, is never independent of their Lord’s power, even for an instant. Recognizing this now awakens the heart before the scene unfolds.

## 99:2

> and the earth throws out its burdens[1],

After the earth is shaken, it brings out its burdens. One interpretation understands these burdens as the dead it has held; another includes the treasures beneath its surface to which people’s hearts became attached. The earth carried people, received their bodies when they were buried, and held the wealth they placed within it, until Allah’s command comes and it returns what was deposited there. Allah says: and casts out all what it contains, and becomes empty.

One explanation for calling its contents burdens is that they are a trust it bears until the time comes to discharge it. This reminds the human being of their own trust: Indeed, We offered the Trust to the heavens, the earth, and the mountains, yet they refused to bear it and were afraid of it. But man assumed it. Allah has given the human being reason, sent revelation and charged them with commands and prohibitions. Life is therefore not mere movement without choice or responsibility. Just as the earth does not retain what it has been commanded to release, the human being will not escape being questioned about what they were charged to do.

Another interpretation calls the treasures burdens because they weighed heavily in people’s estimation: people prized them and competed for them, even though they did not have the value before Allah that people imagined. The Prophetic report reveals how their view of wealth changes. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “The earth will vomit long pieces of its liver like columns of gold and silver”. He then described a murderer coming and saying that he had killed for this, someone who had severed family ties saying that he had done so for it, and a thief saying that his hand had been cut off because of it. They then leave that wealth without taking any of it.

[In the Arabic report, أفلاذ means pieces and الأسطوان means columns.] These are immense treasures in plain sight, yet their abundance does not restore their former hold on people’s hearts. The report itself does not specify that this occurs after resurrection. The lesson is that someone may see the wealth that drove them to shed blood, sever kinship or steal lying before them and no longer want to take it. Do not wait for the illusion to be exposed before correcting your own measure of value.

The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) also said: “What do I have to do with this worldly life? I am in this worldly life like a rider who seeks shade under a tree, then he goes away and leaves it.” A traveler takes the benefit they need without allowing the shade to make them forget their departure. He said as well: “Were this world worth a wing of a mosquito in the sight of Allah, He would not have given the disbeliever a drink of water thereof.” Thus the burdens brought out by the earth connect with the surah’s ending: what carries most weight in people’s estimation is not necessarily greatest before Allah. A deed they considered insignificant may have a more lasting effect on their destiny than treasures for which they sacrificed their religion.

## 99:3

> and man says[2], “What is the matter with it?”

A person sees the earth, once familiar and stable, shaken and bringing out what is within it, and asks: “What is the matter with it?” This is the astonishment of someone who has long walked upon it, pursued their desires there and built their hopes upon it, only to find the whole place transformed. To bring that feeling closer, someone might arrive at work or school and find sweeping changes. They recognize that something important lies behind them and ask what has happened. How much greater is the question when it is the whole earth that has changed, when the buried have come out and its treasures have been exposed? They are asking about the momentous event behind this transformation, not about a passing tremor whose effects they expect to end.

These reports do not become imaginary scenes simply because people choose not to think about them. The Quran describes the astonishment of those raised from the dead: They will say, “Woe to us! Who has resurrected us from our resting place?” [They will be told,] “This is what the Most Compassionate promised, and the messengers told the truth.” What they had ignored has become visible, and what the messengers told them was true before they saw it. The reader therefore faces this question while still able to act, rather than postponing reflection until the shock arrives and the earth itself gives the answer.

## 99:4

> On that Day it will recount all its news,

The answer is that the earth itself will recount all its news. The place where deeds took place in this world becomes a witness to them. Its transformation is not without purpose, and the matter goes beyond revealing what lies underground. The time has come to report what happened upon it—things people forgot, or imagined that burial and concealment had put beyond accountability. The blood of the oppressed that was shed, a vulnerable person killed and buried, someone walking arrogantly in their wrongdoing, stolen wealth hidden away, an inheritance taken unjustly, a word spoken in secret: every one of these has a story that the passage of years cannot erase.

Someone who thought they were hidden in a place where no one could see them discovers that the place itself reports the deed, its doer and its time. Nor is any obedience or good done upon it lost. Allah’s words, Allah has kept their complete record, while they have forgotten it, show the difference between human memory and the Lord’s knowledge. A person may forget words and situations, but forgetting does not remove them from the record. The familiar roles are reversed: the earth once carried people and received their actions, and now it reports on them. Neither the absence of a human witness nor the years that have passed since a sin prove that the matter is over.

## 99:5

> because your Lord has inspired it [to do so].

The earth does not speak of its own accord: because your Lord has inspired it [to do so]. It remained silent at Allah’s command, and at His command it speaks and reports. Its silence when an oppressor walked upon it was neither approval of the oppression nor any lack of power on Allah’s part to seize the wrongdoer. Rather, it bears people until the time its Lord has appointed, then submits to His command.

One interpretation understands revelation here as a swift, hidden command, portraying the earth as awaiting the signal and hastening to obey. [In the Arabic أَوْحَىٰ لَهَا, one interpretation relates the preposition لَـ to a prompt response; another understands a benefit to the earth, as though it has been permitted to release its burdens and disclose what happened upon it.] The wrongs it has carried do not remain without a report. This awakens awareness of how created things are angered for Allah’s sake and harmed by corruption. Of Hell, Allah says: almost bursting in fury. Created things are not, as the heedless imagine, detached from their Creator’s command.

Conversely, the report about the merit of teaching people good tells us that the inhabitants of the heavens and earth pray for the teacher, even the ant in its hole and the fish. The effects of good are not confined to the praise its doer hears from other people. When a funeral passed the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him), he said: “Relieved or relieving?” He explained that a believer finds rest from this world’s toil and harm in Allah’s mercy, whereas people, lands, trees and animals find relief from the wicked person. Wickedness harms those around its doer, and that harm reaches beyond humanity; similarly, the benefits of good extend further than its doer can count.

A related meaning appears in Allah’s words: If Allah were to take people to task for their wrongdoing, He would not leave a single living being on earth. One interpretation sees the delay in punishment as mercy toward the animals that would perish if people were punished at once for their wrongdoing. The earth’s swift obedience also challenges the human being. The solid, silent earth changes at its Lord’s command until it speaks, while a person hears revelation yet postpones responding and refuses to change their way of life. The lesson is not merely to marvel that the earth can speak, but to question one’s own obedience: who responds to the command, and who hears it and carries on heedlessly?

## 99:6

> On that Day all people will come forward in separate groups to be shown their deeds[3].

After the scene of the earth’s testimony, all people will come forward in separate groups to be shown their deeds. People emerge separately, in differing conditions: some faces are bright and radiant, while others are darkened and covered with dust. Each person is absorbed in the outcome of their deeds. To appreciate the intensity of this concern, imagine a deadly disease spreading through a country. Some people have it and others do not, but no one knows their own condition. Everyone is tested, and a short time is fixed for announcing all the results. Men, women and children rush out at once, each wanting reassurance about themselves. Roads and transport fill with people, and few notice anyone else because their own hopes and fears occupy them so completely.

If awaiting news about an illness can do this to the heart, what of awaiting news about a destiny whose consequences do not end when the years of this world pass? People may be distressed as they wait for an examination result, or to learn whether a tumor can be treated or will be difficult to cure. Here, however, they await the outcome of their deeds and their fate in the Hereafter. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) told us that death will be slaughtered and the inhabitants of Paradise and Hell will be told of everlasting life without death. The concern is not for a brief future that will end and be forgotten.

Nor is this gathering limited to one country or to everyone alive on earth at one time. It includes all people, from Adam to the last of them, each facing their own record. This explains why each person is so completely occupied with their own affairs: on that Day everyone will flee from his brother, and On that day, everyone will have enough concern of his own. The scene is more than a crowd of bodies: it is hearts absorbed in their destiny.

[The Arabic لِيُرَوْا أَعْمَالَهُمْ is passive: they are to be shown their deeds.] A person cannot choose to look only at what pleases them and turn away from what distresses them. Deeds they forgot, or whose consequences they never considered while doing them, are displayed. They may have spoken carelessly and acted as though nothing had consequences in this world; now they see what came from them, and disliking it cannot cancel its presentation. This is not a brief paper telling them they passed or failed, after which they leave. It is the appearance of their deeds and the recompense that follows. After the earth is shaken and its news disclosed, attention turns to what each person has brought and to a measure that neglects not even the smallest part of it.

## 99:7

> So whoever does an atom’s weight[4] of good will see it,

The measure reaches down to an atom’s weight: good its doer considered insignificant is not lost. [The Arabic ذَرَّة is an expression of extreme smallness. It has been explained as a tiny ant, or as fine dust visible in a shaft of sunlight.] The point is that a deed’s small size does not remove it from the reckoning. Allah says: We will place the scales of justice on the Day of Resurrection, and no soul will be wronged in the least. Even if a deed is the weight of a mustard seed, We will bring it forth. Sufficient are We as Reckoners. These are scales of justice: they do not disregard a small deed because it is small, or lose what was hidden from everyone except its doer.

To bring this closer, a person might measure their charity in grams of gold or silver, or kilograms of food, then remember that the Quran’s measure reaches an atom’s weight. They should not look at their charity as though only what impresses people counts. Shame at giving a little, when it is all they have, may prevent them from giving at all. They desire good, yet abandon what is within reach because it falls short of the gift they wished to offer. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) therefore taught: “So, protect yourselves from Hellfire even with half a date.” A small gift can benefit its giver before Allah. Waiting to have much must not become a reason to give nothing.

This measure extends beyond money. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Do not belittle any good deed, even if it be meeting your brother with a cheerful face.” A smile offered to a brother may last only moments, yet it is not an overlooked deed. Removing something harmful from the road may likewise seem a small matter to the person who comes across it, but it is charity. The report says: “removing what is harmful from the way is a charity.” Good is not confined to outward actions either. Intending to reconcile people, and sincerely resolving to give and support the religion, are forms of good for which reward is hoped. A servant should attend to the desire for good within the heart as well as the outward form of the deed.

[The Arabic يَرَهُ, ‘will see it,’ has been understood as seeing the deed itself or seeing its recompense.] A person may see both the deed and the reward prepared for it. Their deed has not been forgotten only for them to be told a bare number. Allah says: for We used to record all what you used to do. A servant remembers that their night prayer and struggle with themselves have been preserved, and that they will come to know their reward. The aim is to keep the presentation of deeds and their reward in mind, without asserting a detailed form that the ayah has not explained.

A person’s impatience to know their fate does not cause any part of their account to be omitted. Allah says of those who neglected their duty: They will bear their burdens on their backs. They face the consequences of deeds they carried, not merely stories from long ago. Awareness of the precision of the record makes a person see that every deed they sent ahead deserves attention. What once passed almost unnoticed may become something whose outcome they await anxiously. They remember good from which they hope to benefit, then evil whose effects they fear, until the anxiety of awaiting the result nearly overwhelms their hearts.

This scene affects hearts differently. Someone who did much good hopes to see what will delight them. Someone who committed many sins fears what they sent ahead. Someone who mixed good and bad remains between hope and fear: preparation and neglect do not lead to the same state. The Quran speaks of the length of the standing: on a Day the length of which is fifty thousand years. Such a day is not suited to the carelessness of someone who wants to skim through their record without accounting for it.

One interpretation also recognizes that some consequences of deeds may be brought forward in this world, before the Hereafter. Part of the reward for obedience can be being enabled to obey again, and finding the effects of one’s good in what Allah makes easy. A Hadith states that a believer is given a reward for a good deed in this world and recompensed for it in the Hereafter, whereas a disbeliever is given worldly benefits for their good deeds, until they reach the Hereafter with no good deed left for which to be rewarded there. Benefits such as provision, health and good received by them or their children may thus be brought forward as recompense for the good they did in this life.

Kindness to people is never outside Allah’s knowledge. Yet recompense in the Hereafter is inseparable from faith: good deeds by someone who dies in disbelief do not become a means of final salvation merely because that person received a worldly return. The surah therefore does more than invite admiration for the precision of the measure. It opens the way to action: do the good you can, and do not belittle any of it.

## 99:8

> and whoever does an atom’s weight of evil will see it.

Just as not the smallest particle of good is lost, not the smallest particle of evil disappears. A sin does not become harmless because its doer calls it ‘only a word,’ ‘a small lie’ or ‘a minor violation not worth dwelling on.’ Luqman’s counsel to his son includes: O my dear son, even if a deed were the weight of a mustard seed – whether in a rock or in the heavens or in the earth – Allah will bring it forth. One interpretation understands this as a reminder that Allah will bring forth a sin however tiny or hidden its location. Even if its doer hides inside a rock in a mountain, or seeks some remote place for it, they cannot leave Allah’s earth, dominion or knowledge. Concealment does not remove a deed’s consequences, any more than smallness in its doer’s eyes does.

This is particularly clear in the danger of the tongue. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “Indeed a man may utter a statement that he does not see any harm in, but for which he will fall seventy autumns in the Fire.” The speaker does not consider the word serious, yet its consequence can reach this terrible extent. [Seventy autumns in the Arabic narration means seventy years.] That can approach an entire earthly lifetime. One illustration is a word spoken before an unjust ruler that leads to a Muslim’s blood being shed. Its consequences are not measured by its number of letters or the few moments it took to utter.

The same applies to circulating rumors without verification or thinking well of others. Someone hears a statement and repeats it, or reads a report and passes it on, then is surprised to be accountable for what they regard as merely relaying information. Concerning the incident in which Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her) was falsely accused, Allah says: when you were passing it from one tongue to the other, and saying with your mouths what you had no knowledge of, and were taking it lightly, whereas with Allah it was a grave offense. 

Speech is not judged by what a gathering has grown used to or tongues find easy to say, but by its reality before Allah and the harm it causes. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) therefore warned Aisha (may Allah be pleased with her): “beware of (evil) deeds that are regarded as insignificant”. He reminded her that these deeds are subject to accountability before Allah. This concerns bad deeds their doer considers insignificant. One must not repeat them habitually, reassured by the harmless label one has given them.

In a report of Ibn Masud’s own words, a believer sees their sins as though sitting beneath a mountain they fear will fall upon them, whereas a wicked person regards them as a fly passing over their nose and brushes it away. The difference is not that the wicked person is completely unable to recognize something unpleasant—a fly is unpleasant too—but that they treat its effect as something removed by a gesture, without concern in the heart. The believer remembers the greatness of the One they disobeyed and the seriousness of standing before Him. A person may know that an income is unlawful yet minimize the matter because of the money it yields. They need a measure that restores to its true size what desire has magnified.

It is not enough for the heart to be afraid for a moment while life carries on unchanged. Applying this lesson includes setting one’s waking time around Fajr prayer, not work alone; weighing a statement before publishing it; and asking whether one’s earnings and food are lawful instead of taking them carelessly. A person strives according to their ability, as Allah says: So fear Allah as much as you can. The precision of the reckoning must not make them abandon effort, and the limits of ability must not become an excuse for carelessness.

The Quran is revealed to change conduct, not merely to give a person pleasant recitation while leaving heedlessness intact. It develops an inward restraint that prevents a believer from oppressing others even when they could deceive people, because they know they cannot deceive Allah. Those whom admonition does not restrain may still require the lawful authority’s restraint in accordance with religious law. Faith’s wakefulness, however, prevents sin where human supervision cannot reach. Thus the surah’s ending brings the earthquake’s lesson back to today’s conduct: do not belittle any good within your power, or minimize an evil your desires make attractive. Prepare to see deeds of which nothing has been lost.
