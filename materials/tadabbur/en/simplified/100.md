## 100:1

> By the galloping, panting horses[1],

“By the galloping, panting horses[1]”. The surah opens with movement, not stillness. Two interpretations of al-‘ādiyāt [Arabic: الْعَادِيَاتِ] have been mentioned: camels on the pilgrimage and horses galloping in battle in Allah’s cause. The image of horses is the main one used to develop the reflection here; ḍabḥ [Arabic: ضَبْحًا] is the sound of their breathing as they gallop hard. The surah portrays a creature giving all its energy to the task for which it has been put to use. It is as though the worth of the means lies less in the means themselves than in the purpose they serve.

## 100:2

> striking sparks of fire [with their hooves][2],

Then comes “striking sparks of fire [with their hooves][2]”. Hooves strike rock and throw off sparks, yet the movement does not stop. The sparks appear in the darkness along the way, a sign of continued progress amid obstacles. The repeated Arabic connector fa [Arabic: فَ] also gives the scene a sense of speed and continuity: one act of obedience follows another, rather than an undertaking that begins only to fall apart whenever it encounters a difficulty.

Sources: QuranEnc · 100:2 · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100/2; QuranEnc · 100:2 — [2] While galloping over rocky terrain. · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100#2

## 100:3

> launching raids at dawn[3],

The words “launching raids at dawn[3]” portray the travellers reaching the morning while still carrying out their task; the night spent on the road has not caused them to give up. The ayah does not glorify exhaustion for its own sake. It brings out the meaning of continuing until the work reaches its proper destination, without waiting for ideal circumstances that never come.

Sources: QuranEnc · 100:3 · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100/3; QuranEnc · 100:3 — [3] While the enemy is unaware of their raid. · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100#3

## 100:4

> stirring up thereby clouds of dust,

“stirring up thereby clouds of dust”. The dust itself has become a visible trace of movement. Serving the truth is not always a clean, comfortable undertaking that costs nothing. It may involve fatigue, upheaval and hardship, just as galloping raises dust. Someone who insists on fulfilling their mission without sacrificing any comfort has not yet entered into the picture of effort drawn by this oath.

## 100:5

> plunging thereby into the midst of the enemy[4],

“plunging thereby into the midst of the enemy[4]”. The movement reaches the heart of the battlefield, not its safe edge. The surah does not mention the outcome of the battle here: neither victory nor defeat nor spoils. Its purpose is to bring out wholehearted effort and reaching the place where one’s duty must be carried out. A person may be held accountable for faithfully following the path entrusted to them before seeing the outcome they hoped for.

## 100:6

> indeed, man is very ungrateful to his Lord[5] –

After this scene of unstinting effort comes the reversal: “indeed, man is very ungrateful to his Lord[5]”. Kanūd [Arabic: كَنُودٌ] describes someone deeply ungrateful: blessings reach them but do not bear their proper fruit, like land that receives water yet produces no growth. A person may be quick to count what they have missed out on and slow to count what they have been given. Being surrounded by blessings then becomes a constant feeling of lack, instead of gratitude and action.

## 100:7

> and he himself is a witness to that[6] –

The words “and he himself is a witness to that[6]” place a person before testimony they cannot easily erase. One interpretation is that people bear witness against themselves through what they know of their own condition; another is that Allah is the witness against them. Both meanings leave no room for manufactured excuses. People often know the standard well when judging others, then try to forget it when that judgment concerns themselves.

Sources: QuranEnc · 100:7 · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100/7; QuranEnc · 100:7 — [6] By his words and actions. · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100#7

## 100:8

> and he is truly extreme in his love of wealth.

“and he is truly extreme in his love of wealth”. Here, al-khayr [Arabic: الْخَيْرِ], literally good, refers to wealth. The problem is not having money or benefiting from it, but a love that grips the heart until its owner becomes bound to their possessions and is held back from giving and taking action. The greatest obstacles can therefore be internal rather than external: fear for one’s comfort, wealth or self can make a person ungrateful even when opportunities for gratitude lie open before them.

## 100:9

> Does he not know that when the contents of graves will be overturned,

The words “Does he not know that when the contents of graves will be overturned” turn our attention from attachment to what we hold in our hands to a scene in which what lies in the earth is overturned. Whatever a person has tried to preserve will not prevent them from becoming a body in a grave, then being brought out of it to face the reckoning. The use of mā [Arabic: مَا], meaning what, in this portrayal further conveys how people are stripped of the titles through which they once magnified their own importance in this world.

Sources: QuranEnc · 100:9 · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/100/9

## 100:10

> and the secrets of the hearts will be brought to light[7]?

Resurrection does not only bring bodies out of their graves: “and the secrets of the hearts will be brought to light[7]”. Whatever has mingled in the heart—sincerity and showing off, fear and desire, love of the truth or love of oneself—is drawn out and made apparent, as an extract is drawn from within something. It is therefore not enough for a person’s outward activity to resemble the scene of the galloping horses. Someone may do a great deal while their intention remains fixed on praise or gain. The surah brings all deeds back to what lies within the heart.

## 100:11

> Indeed, their Lord is All-Aware of them on that Day[8].

The words “Indeed, their Lord is All-Aware of them on that Day[8]” close with a truth that puts both effort and ingratitude in perspective: Allah knows the effort that people did not see, the ingratitude its owner concealed, and the motive behind moving forward or holding back. No trace of a sincere step is lost, and a great deal of activity cannot deceive the One who knows what hearts conceal. In this way, the surah’s measure moves from the appearance of an action to the reality of the person performing it before Allah.
