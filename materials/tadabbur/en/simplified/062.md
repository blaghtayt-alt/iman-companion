## 62:1

> All that is in the heavens and all that is on earth glorifies Allah, the Sovereign, the Most Holy, the All-Mighty, the All-Wise.

[The Arabic present-tense verb for glorifying Allah here portrays worship that continues without ceasing.] Everything in the heavens and on earth glorifies Allah; those who turn away or leave do Him no harm. It is the servant who needs the reward of this glorification and to live in harmony with an obedient universe. The surah opens with the names ‘the Sovereign, the Most Holy, the All-Mighty, the All-Wise’, affirming that all sovereignty belongs to Him: He is free of every imperfection, mighty beyond defeat, and wise in His commands and choices. One effect of believing in Allah’s name ‘the Most Holy’ is that a servant strives to cleanse and purify their own soul for Allah, instead of leaving it captive to its ailments.

## 62:2

> It is He Who has sent among the unlettered [Arabs] a messenger from among themselves, reciting to them His verses and purifying them, and teaching them the Book and Wisdom [i.e., Sunnah], although before that they were clearly misguided.

The perfection of Allah’s sovereignty, might and wisdom is expressed in His sending a messenger from among the unlettered people themselves. Through him, a people without a revealed book became a community entrusted with scripture and revelation. The mission is clear: to recite the verses to people so that they hear their Lord’s words, purify their souls of the attitudes and ideas of the time of ignorance, and teach them the Book and wisdom. The mention of purification before teaching shows that knowledge entering an uncleansed heart can become an instrument of arrogance or corruption. The Quran removes the impurity, establishes a new outlook, and teaches the details of the path. Gratitude for revelation includes an Arab learning the language of their scripture and understanding its meanings, rather than devoting every ability to worldly life while remaining content to be ignorant of Allah’s words.

## 62:3

> And [he is also sent] to others of them who have not yet joined them, and He is the All-Mighty, the All-Wise.

The gift of the prophetic mission was not confined to the first generation or to Arabs. The verse also mentions ‘others of them who have not yet joined them’: they join through Islam and follow the same path. One interpretation identifies them as non-Arabs; another includes the generation after the Companions and those who came later. The Hadith concerning Salman al-Farisi shows that some people seek faith even when it seems far beyond reach, striving until they attain it. The door remains open: a person learns, purifies their soul and acts upon what they know, without making the length of the journey or a different ancestry an excuse for failing to set out.

## 62:4

> That is the bounty of Allah that He gives to whom He wills, for Allah is the Lord of great bounty.

Prophethood, the choice of those entrusted with the message, and the ability to follow those who went before are all gifts from Allah, bestowed upon whom He wills. A servant should not rule out being raised by Allah after having been far away, nor claim personal credit for the good Allah has enabled them to do. His bounty is immense and wide. For someone who turns to Him sincerely and strives to seek faith and live by it, the door is not closed.

## 62:5

> The similitude of those who were given the Torah but failed to uphold it[1], is like that of a donkey laden with books[2]. How terrible is the similitude of those who reject the verses of Allah! And Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people.

The words ‘were given the Torah’ refer to being charged with the Torah and entrusted with preserving, understanding and applying it. Yet those described did not fulfill that trust properly. Failings in understanding and practice turned knowledge into a burden that did not change its bearer. Hence the comparison with a donkey carrying books: many books rest on its back, but their contents do not shape its conduct. What matters is neither the amount of information nor pride in possessing it, but whether the verses produce reverence, change and action. Someone who learns the Quran and then neglects to apply it should fear carrying the burden of knowledge without receiving its light.

## 62:6

> Say, “O you who are Jews, if you claim that you alone are Allah’s allies apart from all other people, then wish for death, if you are truthful.

The claim to be an ally of Allah with a special standing before Him is tested by one’s relationship with the Hereafter. True closeness to Allah brings a longing to meet Him, rather than attachment to worldly life simply for its own sake. Hence the challenge: ‘then wish for death, if you are truthful’. Death is not being sought for its own sake. Rather, claiming closeness to Allah while the heart desires nothing but remaining in this world exposes the contradiction between the claim and the reality.

## 62:7

> But they will never wish for it because of what their hands have sent forth[3]. And Allah is All-Knowing of the wrongdoers.

Their refusal to wish for death is connected to what they have done. A person may know things about themselves that make them dread the reckoning, however grand their spoken claims. Allah knows the wrongdoers: titles and affiliations cannot conceal the reality of deeds, and claims cannot create closeness to Him when the heart knows what it has sent ahead.

## 62:8

> Say, “The death from which you run away will surely overcome you, then you will be brought back to the Knower of the unseen and the seen, and He will inform you of what you used to do.”

The death that a person avoids remembering, or tries to postpone by every worldly means, will inevitably meet them. Love of this world and dislike of death can lead someone to abandon the truth they know and fail to act upon it. They will then return to the Knower of the unseen and the seen. He knows what the heart concealed just as He knows the actions that were visible, and will tell His servant what they actually did, not merely what they knew or claimed.

## 62:9

> O you who believe, when the call for prayer is made on Friday, then hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trading. That is better for you, if only you knew.

Allah has set aside a time each week when worship takes precedence over trading and earning. When the call to Friday prayer is made, even the sale a merchant has been waiting for must be left behind in order to go to the remembrance of Allah. This is more than a movement of the feet: after being occupied with worldly affairs, the heart turns with longing toward learning and prayer. Gathering is itself part of the purpose. People come together for remembrance and prayer to receive a shared standard of guidance to carry back into their work, so that religion does not become something separate from life.

## 62:10

> When the prayer is over, disperse in the land and seek from the bounty of Allah, and remember Allah much so that you may be successful.

Once prayer is over, Islam does not command people to abandon worldly life. Instead, it says: ‘disperse in the land and seek from the bounty of Allah’. Remembering Allah, working and earning thus belong within the same day. Dispersing does not mean forgetting what was heard, which is why the verse also says: ‘remember Allah much’. A person returns to their trade and work carrying the guidance they received, applying it in buying, selling and the rest of life. This is an orderly dispersal after gathering to learn, not a separation between the mosque and everyday reality.

## 62:11

> When they see some merchandise or amusement, they rush towards it and leave you standing. Say, “That which is with Allah is better than amusement and merchandise, and Allah is the Best of Providers.”

Breaking away for trade or amusement is the opposite of the intended gathering: a disordered movement in which worldly pressure draws people away from remembrance. Some reports in the works of Quranic interpretation mention that this happened amid severe need and high prices. Even so, the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) remained standing, with those willing to make the sacrifice beside him, preserving a nucleus to which those who left could return. People must not all disperse whenever a trading opportunity appears or economic pressure grows. If they do, who will carry concern for the religion and remain there for people? The heart draws steadiness from ‘That which is with Allah is better’ and ‘Allah is the Best of Providers’. When this certainty is firmly rooted, a person can leave amusement and immediate profit for what is with Allah.
