## 93:1

> By the morning sunlight,

The Surah opens with the morning sunlight, when light spreads and activity begins after the stillness of night. Its first word thus suggests that a period of darkness is not the end. One authentic report relates that Gabriel was slow to come to the Prophet ﷺ; the polytheists said that his Lord had abandoned him, and the Surah was revealed. Another relates that he ﷺ became ill and did not stand in night prayer for two or three nights. A polytheist woman then taunted him, claiming that his devil had left him, and these verses were revealed. 

Neither report establishes that the delay in revelation caused his illness. The morning light is therefore more than a beautiful scene: it evokes light returning after a sense of interruption. The effect of the Quran on a person also appears in Ibn Abbas’s report that the Prophet ﷺ was the most generous of people, and was especially generous in Ramadan when Gabriel studied the Quran with him. Revelation does more than convey information; it changes the heart and conduct. 

It has also been said that the morning is a time for activity and giving, while the night is a time for stillness and being alone with Allah. A believer needs both: time to work and benefit others, and time to turn back to their Lord. Even when a visible light disappears or a star fades, revelation’s guidance is not measured by that change. A comparable image appears in Allah’s words: «By the star when it sets» and «Your companion [i.e., the Prophet] has neither strayed nor is he deluded». They affirm that the Prophet’s guidance does not go out as celestial lights disappear.

## 93:2

> and by the night when it covers with darkness[1],

«and by the night when it covers with darkness» evokes the night as it grows still and seems so prolonged that a person feels it has stopped moving. This resembles moments of trial when time seems frozen and no end to the pain is visible. Yet the night is not a time without purpose. It has been understood as a time for stillness, rest and being alone with Allah, just as the morning is a time for activity. Each condition calls for its own form of worship: gratitude in ease, patience in trial, action when strong, and refuge in Allah and steadfastness when weak. 

Hence the command: «Lord of the heavens and earth and all that is between them. So worship Him and be constant in worshiping Him». Do not wait for perfect circumstances before worshiping Allah. The Quran helps the heart through this night. A story is told of a Palestinian prisoner who said that the Quran, remembrance of Allah and the example of the prophets’ patience helped him and his companions remain steadfast in prison. This is an illustrative account, not a Prophetic report. 

The meaning it illustrates is affirmed in the Quran: «so that We may strengthen your heart thereby», and «We relate to you [O Prophet] the stories of the messengers to strengthen your heart». The night may be long, but the believer’s task is to maintain the connection that enables them to await the morning, rather than cease striving.

## 93:3

> your Lord has neither forsaken you [O Prophet] nor hated you;

After the oath comes the answer the heart needed: «your Lord has neither forsaken you [O Prophet] nor hated you». His Lord has neither left him nor hated him. An authentic account of the revelation relates that the Prophet ﷺ was ill and did not stand in night prayer for two or three nights. A polytheist woman taunted him, claiming that his devil had abandoned him, and these verses were revealed. The wording itself is tender. [In the Arabic phrase «مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ», ‘your Lord has not forsaken you,’ Allah is described as his Lord: the One who nurtures and cares for him. In «وَمَا قَلَىٰ», ‘nor hated,’ the object pronoun meaning ‘you’ is not repeated. One reflection on this omission is that the word for hatred is not joined in the wording to the pronoun referring to the Prophet ﷺ. This is an interpretive observation, not the only explanation for omitting the object.] A delay in what you love does not mean that Allah has forgotten you. 

When the Prophet ﷺ asked Gabriel, «What makes you not visit us more than you do?», Allah revealed: «We do not descend except with the command of your Lord». The same verse ends: «Your Lord is never forgetful». A supplication attributed to the Prophet ﷺ after Taif includes: «If You are not angry with me, then I care not». Its chain of transmission was judged weak by Al-Albani, so it must not be presented as an established Prophetic saying. The lesson drawn here is that, amid pain, the deeper concern is whether one is following a path pleasing to Allah, rather than why events have not followed one’s wishes. 

Abraham was in the fire, the Prophet ﷺ in the cave, Joseph in prison, Jonah in the belly of the fish, and Job in illness. Their difficult circumstances did not mean that Allah had abandoned them.

## 93:4

> the Hereafter is better for you than the present life;

«the Hereafter is better for you than the present life» first conveys its clear meaning: the Hereafter is better for the Prophet ﷺ than this world. Allah’s favor cannot be measured by what is missed in a brief moment. Another interpretation is that the later stages of his life and mission would be better than the earlier ones. The weakness suffered in Makkah would not be the final scene; Madinah, the spread of the religion and the completion of the message would follow. 

This does not mean that every worldly moment would be comfortable. It teaches the heart not to confine its view to a single episode. A believer’s share of Allah’s protection and support grows with their following of the Prophet ﷺ. Allah says: «O Prophet, Allah is sufficient for you and for the believers who follow you». On this interpretation, Allah is sufficient for His Prophet and for those who follow him. When a trial lasts, do not let the present moment define your whole life. 

There is a real Hereafter better than this life, and there are stages on the path of faith that you cannot yet see. Continue in obedience and in following the Prophet, so that today’s distress does not conceal the promise of what lies ahead and of the Hereafter.

## 93:5

> and your Lord will certainly give you so much that you will be well pleased.

«and your Lord will certainly give you so much that you will be well pleased» opens the prospect of giving without specifying a single gift. [The Arabic verb «يُعْطِيكَ», ‘give you,’ does not name what is given.] The forms of giving extend beyond one list: Quran, victory, conquest, intercession, an honored station, Paradise and good for the community. Then the outcome of that giving is stated: his satisfaction. This may be considered alongside Moses’s words: «I hastened to You, my Lord, to please You». 

Moses hastens in pursuit of his Lord’s pleasure; here the Lord promises the Prophet ﷺ gifts that will please him. Contentment does not mean that what Allah decrees will always match one’s wishes. Speaking about the Messenger ﷺ among the believers, the Quran says: «If he were to obey you in many matters, you would surely suffer». This verse can also help us reflect on why human wishes alone do not determine what is beneficial: a person cannot see all that is hidden, and mercy may lie within something they presently dislike. 

Such was Job’s trial, after which Allah relieved his distress «as a mercy from Us and as a reminder for Our worshipers». Among the clearest accounts of the Prophet’s concern for his community is the report of his weeping for them. Allah told Gabriel to convey to him: «We will please you with regard to your Ummah and will not displease you». Just as a father feels reassured when he sees his children’s affairs set right and no longer fears for them, one may reflect on the Prophet’s joy at the religion’s spread and people entering it in multitudes, as described in Surah An-Nasr. 

The verse is not a promise of one gift alone. It teaches trust in a Lord whose gifts are inexhaustible, and contentment that comes from trusting His wisdom rather than insisting on one’s own picture of His giving.

## 93:6

> Did He not find you an orphan then take good care of you?

When a trial lasts, a person may become trapped in the present, forgetting Allah’s earlier kindness and overlooking what may still come. After promising future good, the Surah therefore reminds the Prophet ﷺ of the past: «Did He not find you an orphan then take good care of you?» Before you possessed any means of helping yourself, Allah cared for you. Every human being begins in darkness, unable to secure anything for themselves, as Allah says: «He creates you in the wombs of your mothers [in stages], creation after creation, in three layers of darkness». 

His father died before the Prophet ﷺ could see him; his mother died while he was young, then his grandfather, and he passed into his uncle’s care. In later stages he lost other loved ones and left the Makkah he loved. Those later bereavements and his departure from Makkah occurred after this Surah’s revelation, yet Allah’s care continued through every transition. [The Arabic «فَآوَىٰ» means that He gave him shelter; the word evokes security and reassurance, beyond merely feeding him or making him free of need.] When you face a problem and cannot see its solution, do not limit Allah’s power to the means visible to you. 

Remember earlier occasions when He cared for you before you knew how matters would be resolved. Take the steps available to you, and let the memory of His kindness strengthen your patience and trust.

## 93:7

> Did He not find you unguided then guide you?

«Did He not find you unguided then guide you?» does not mean that the Prophet ﷺ worshiped idols or lived in the misguidance of disbelief. The meaning explained here is that, before the Quran was revealed, he did not know revelation’s detailed guidance or the way Allah wished to be worshiped. He saw the corruption of idolatry and the distortions that had affected the People of the Book. He sought solitude, devoted himself to worship and searched for truth. 

Yet knowledge of Allah’s will in detail cannot be obtained through the natural disposition alone. Revelation came and showed him the way. This helps explain the weight of uncertainty on someone who loves Allah and wishes to please Him but does not know how. It also offers a way of reflecting on the next Surah: «Have We not reassured your heart for you [O Prophet]» and «and relieved you of your burden». Finding the path after searching for it is one way a burden can be lifted. 

This is one reflective connection, not an exclusive explanation of those two verses. The verse about love gives a clear direction: «If you love Allah then follow me; Allah will love you». Someone seeking nearness to Allah is not left to invent their own worship. Guidance has come through revelation and the Prophet’s Sunnah. Relief begins when the question of what Allah wants of us becomes a known path we can follow.

## 93:8

> Did He not find you in need then enrich you?

«Did He not find you in need then enrich you?» recalls a third blessing. [The Arabic «عَائِلًا» describes someone poor and in need.] Allah enriched His Prophet ﷺ after poverty, so that his life combined patient endurance in need with gratitude in prosperity. Richness is not confined to money. It is also a blessing to be given companions with whom one finds a home and who help one along the path. Allah gave His Prophet the companionship of Abu Bakr As-Siddiq and others who shared the work of conveying the message; later, after the Surah’s revelation, He gave him refuge in Madinah among the Ansar. 

A person may possess money yet remain in need of a sincere companion, or have little money while being rich in guidance, peace of heart and good company. After recalling three blessings—shelter, guidance and relief from need—the Surah turns to three practical responsibilities. A blessing in the Quran is not merely a comforting memory; it is a trust. What Allah has given you should affect those around you. If He sheltered you, do not make the vulnerable feel oppressed. If He guided you, do not drive away someone asking for the way. If He enriched you with His favors, do not live as though you had received nothing.

## 93:9

> So do not mistreat the orphan,

The first practical expression of gratitude is: «So do not mistreat the orphan». You cannot provide an orphan with the complete care that Allah provides, but you can refrain from adding oppression to their loss. Do not exploit the vulnerability of someone without a father by belittling them, denying their rights or breaking their heart. The connection with «Did He not find you an orphan then take good care of you?» turns gratitude into conduct: mercy you have received becomes mercy you show to someone else. This principle extends beyond orphans. If Allah has given you wealth, let others benefit from it. If He has given you health, use it to assist someone unable to do what you can. If He has given you knowledge, use it to open a path for someone who needs it. A blessing does not flourish by being confined to its recipient. Its good becomes apparent when it makes that person more merciful toward those enduring what they once endured, or something harder.

## 93:10

> nor repulse the beggar[2];

Then comes the command: «nor repulse the beggar». The person asking includes someone seeking money and, in another application of the verse, someone seeking knowledge and guidance. Both approach you in need, so do not meet them with humiliation. This corresponds to the blessing: «Did He not find you unguided then guide you?» Having been guided by Allah, do not make a seeker regret asking for the path. The Prophet ﷺ accordingly welcomed delegations who came to learn. 

When the delegation of Abd Al-Qays arrived, he welcomed them and said: «Neither will you have disgrace nor will you regret». Coming to learn the way of knowledge and religion is not a cause for disgrace or regret. The Quran affirms the same spirit by instructing the Prophet to say to those who believe in Allah’s verses: «Peace be upon you. Your Lord has decreed mercy upon Himself». Someone approaching religion or seeking knowledge first needs to encounter mercy and peace, rather than an angry face that drives them away. Just as Allah has brought you relief through guidance, let the way you teach others help them find that relief.

## 93:11

> and proclaim the favors of your Lord[3].

The Surah ends with: «and proclaim the favors of your Lord». This does not mean boasting to others. It means recognizing the blessing, thanking Allah for it, showing its effect and directing others to its source. The greatest blessing running through the Surah is the Quran itself. The Prophet ﷺ felt the absence of revelation when its interruption was beyond his control. How, then, can a person choose to abandon a Quran readily at hand and fail to feel what they are missing? 

The Prophet ﷺ said: «There should be no envy except in two (cases)». He described a man whom Allah taught the Quran, who recites it night and day, and a man whom Allah gave wealth, who spends it rightly. The aspiration is to have the same opportunity to do good, without wishing that the other person lose their blessing. It concerns what is done with the Quran or wealth, not merely possessing either. Acknowledging blessings also means not letting all one’s speech become complaint. 

Jacob, peace be upon him, said: «I only complain of my anguish and sorrow to Allah». Al-Hasan Al-Basri is reported to have explained «indeed, man is very ungrateful to his Lord» as counting one’s misfortunes and forgetting one’s blessings. The Surah reverses that habit: remember shelter, guidance and relief from need, then speak of what Allah has given you. Let your tongue bear witness to gratitude rather than become a record of complaints.
