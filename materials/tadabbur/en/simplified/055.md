## 55:1

> The Most Compassionate,

The passage opens with the name ‘The Most Compassionate’, a magnificent name belonging to Allah alone, glorified and exalted is He. Before the blessings that follow are mentioned, it opens the heart to hope and to seeking refuge in Him. Beginning with this name establishes that the teaching, creation, the placing of creation at our service, and balance described next are all expressions of His mercy towards His creatures. Mercy is more than worldly gifts. Among its greatest forms are Allah making Himself known to human beings, opening the way of guidance to them, and giving them what protects their natural disposition, reason, and life from corruption.

## 55:2

> has taught the Qur’an,

The first blessing mentioned after the name of the Most Compassionate is the teaching of the Quran: guidance through revelation takes precedence in value over all other blessings. Human beings did not produce the Quran themselves. Allah spoke it, then made it possible for them to learn it, understand it, and take heed of it. Mentioning the teaching of the Quran before the creation of the human being draws attention to the purpose of human existence before its chronological order. Human beings were created to know their Lord and be guided by His words; the further they turn from revelation, the more they fall short of their full human potential. One of the greatest mercies is to have the door to the Quran opened to us. This Book guides our reason, our hearts, and our standards of judgment, giving us the light by which to understand ourselves, this world, and the Hereafter.

## 55:3

> He created man,

Being created is itself a tremendous blessing. Yet mentioning creation after the teaching of the Quran connects human existence with its purpose and direction, rather than leaving it as a merely physical life detached from guidance. Revelation can raise a person to the highest levels of moral excellence, while turning away from it can corrupt their natural disposition. This is why the Quran uses comparisons with animals to describe those who turn away from its signs when they lose the guidance and insight that distinguish them. Human worth does not lie merely in existing or being able to move and speak. It lies in using what Allah has given us for the purpose for which we were created, and in remaining connected to the light taught by the Most Compassionate.

## 55:4

> and taught him speech.

Clear expression means more than the ability to speak. It includes understanding meanings, distinguishing things and connecting them, then expressing what one has understood and conveying it to others. This ability continues the gift of knowledge through which Allah taught Adam the names; it enables human beings to learn, draw conclusions, explain, and discover. Yet eloquence alone does not mean this blessing is being fully used as it should be: speech can also be false or misleading. The highest use of expression is to convey the truth, and its noblest purpose is to understand the Quran and teach it, allowing the light of revelation to guide our thought and speech. When expression is detached from truth, it becomes a means of deception and corruption instead of an expression of mercy.

## 55:5

> The sun and the moon follow their precise courses,

The sun and moon move according to a precise reckoning. Their regularity is a clear mercy, for life depends on established measures and consistent laws. If the laws of the universe changed every day, human life, knowledge, and work could have no stability. The distances, times, temperatures, and other measures on which the world depends are governed by Allah’s decree, rather than by chaos. The verse does not need to state explicitly who made the sun and moon follow this reckoning: our natural disposition recognizes that human beings neither made nor control this order. It is part of Allah’s mercy that He has given the world an order within which we can live, learn its patterns, and work with them.

## 55:6

> and the stars[1] and trees prostrate [to Allah][2].

[One explanation of the Arabic النَّجْمُ is plants without an upright stem, while الشَّجَرُ refers to plants with a trunk or stem.] Both submit to Allah and prostrate to Him, glorified is He, in the manner He has willed. Just as the heavenly bodies follow a precise reckoning, earthly creation is subject to His command and placed at His service. Creatures that are not charged with moral responsibility follow the course Allah has appointed for them. Human beings, however, have been entrusted with responsibility and given the ability to choose after the path of guidance has been made clear to them. Their responsibility is therefore greater: they see the universe around them obeying its Lord, and are asked to choose to worship and serve Him knowingly and willingly, rather than depart from this universal submission.

## 55:7

> He raised the heaven and enjoined justice

Allah raised the heaven and established the balance for His creatures. The balance has been explained as justice, and also as including the means by which things are weighed and their measures determined. Just as the universe depends on order, human life cannot be sound without a standard for rights, judgments, and ways of understanding. Human beings are morally responsible and can follow guidance or act wickedly, so they need a balance that brings their choices back to what is right. One lesson is that the heavens and earth stand upon justice, and that corrupting justice and reversing sound standards corrupt people’s lives. Possessing ability is not enough: we also need to know where to use it and by what standard to judge.

## 55:8

> so that you may not transgress the limits of justice.

The balance was established to prevent transgression. When people abandon the standard Allah has revealed, the way they describe and judge things begins to go wrong: the treacherous may be trusted and the trustworthy accused of betrayal; the undeserving may be promoted and the qualified excluded. Standards of good and evil, truth and falsehood, are turned upside down. Neglecting the trust placed in us is one of the clearest forms of this reversal. When responsibility is given to those unfit to bear it, the consequences are harmful even if the same titles and slogans remain. Preserving the balance is therefore much more than a narrow matter of personal conduct. It protects society from becoming a place ruled by desire and power without justice.

## 55:9

> Therefore establish weights in justice, and do not give short measure.

We are called to do more than acknowledge that a balance exists: we must uphold it justly without leaving out any of its standards. This includes fairness in people’s rights and judging people, events, and actions by what Allah has taught, rather than by desires, sheer numbers, or attractive appearances. What is true and what is false? What is bad, even if its abundance impresses people? What is a good life? How should responsibilities be entrusted to those qualified for them? All of this requires a balance. Human beings, who have accepted the trust, cannot set their lives right through justice without knowledge, or knowledge without justice. Together, knowledge and justice protect our natural disposition and society, making the gifts of expression and choice a path to mercy rather than corruption.
