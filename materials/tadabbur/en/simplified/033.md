## 33:9

> ﴿ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ ٱذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَةَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ إِذْ جَآءَتْكُمْ جُنُودٌۭ فَأَرْسَلْنَا عَلَيْهِمْ رِيحًۭا وَجُنُودًۭا لَّمْ تَرَوْهَا ۚ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرًا ﴾

> O you who believe, remember Allah’s favor upon you when [enemy] forces came against you[7], but We sent against them a violent wind and forces that you could not see[8]. And Allah is All-Seeing of what you do.

The Qur’an asks believers to reconsider an event they themselves experienced, this time in the light of Allah’s guidance: ﴿اذْكُرُوا نِعْمَةَ اللَّهِ عَلَيْكُمْ﴾. Satan may bring back the past in a way that leaves a person seeing only fear and pain, whereas faith enables them to see Allah’s protection and care at work through events. The confederates arrived in visible armies, but Allah sent against them a wind and forces the believers could not see. This teaches us not to attribute everything to visible causes alone: behind them is Allah, who brings those causes into being and determines their course. The Battle of the Confederates involved relatively little fighting; its great struggle was within people’s hearts. Hence the verse ends with ﴿وَكَانَ اللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرًا﴾. A trial may not be intended to secure a particular worldly gain for the believer, but to reveal how they respond when besieged and whether they emerge with firmer faith.

## 33:10

> ﴿ إِذْ جَآءُوكُم مِّن فَوْقِكُمْ وَمِنْ أَسْفَلَ مِنكُمْ وَإِذْ زَاغَتِ ٱلْأَبْصَـٰرُ وَبَلَغَتِ ٱلْقُلُوبُ ٱلْحَنَاجِرَ وَتَظُنُّونَ بِٱللَّهِ ٱلظُّنُونَا۠ ﴾

> [Remember] when they came upon you from above you and from below you[9], and your eyes grew wild [in horror], and the hearts reached the throats, and you began to entertain various thoughts about Allah[10].

The verse describes no ordinary encounter with an enemy, but encirclement and overwhelming pressure from above and below. ﴿زَاغَتِ الْأَبْصَارُ﴾ evokes eyes that cannot remain steady in extreme panic, while ﴿بَلَغَتِ الْقُلُوبُ الْحَنَاجِرَ﴾ depicts fear reaching its utmost intensity. The verse then moves from bodily reactions to what lies deepest within a person: ﴿وَتَظُنُّونَ بِاللَّهِ الظُّنُونَا﴾. The real danger is that pressure may turn into doubt about the path of faith and Allah’s promise. The ordeal was especially severe because the confederates came after Uhud: the memory of defeat remained vivid, making the possibility of another collapse feel very real. When panic overwhelms us, major decisions should not be made in the turmoil of the moment. We should turn to prayer until the heart becomes calm and our outlook is guided by our relationship with Allah rather than by fear.

## 33:11

> ﴿ هُنَالِكَ ٱبْتُلِىَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَزُلْزِلُوا۟ زِلْزَالًۭا شَدِيدًۭا ﴾

> There the believers were tested and were severely shaken.

﴿هُنَالِكَ﴾ marks the moment of the real test, when pressure becomes so intense that it reveals what hearts contain. The shaking resembles what happens to trees: weak leaves fall, a tree without roots cannot withstand it, and one with deep roots remains firm. Faith is similar. A small doubt may unsettle a heart that has not been nourished by the Qur’an, while a believer may emerge from the same ordeal with forms of devotion that would never have appeared in easier times. A severe trial is not evidence that Allah has abandoned believers. It may bring out patience, dependence on Him and trust in His goodness that elevate them, while exposing hypocrisy and weakness. The roots of faith should therefore be strengthened before the upheaval arrives, rather than waiting until a crisis to begin growing them.

## 33:12

> ﴿ وَإِذْ يَقُولُ ٱلْمُنَـٰفِقُونَ وَٱلَّذِينَ فِى قُلُوبِهِم مَّرَضٌۭ مَّا وَعَدَنَا ٱللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُۥٓ إِلَّا غُرُورًۭا ﴾

> And [remember] when the hypocrites and those with sickness in their hearts said, “Allah and His Messenger did not promise us but delusion!”

When the ordeal intensified, what had remained hidden in times of ease came to light. A hypocrite who speaks the language of faith in ordinary circumstances may lose restraint under trial and voice objections. Here, the first thing to emerge was an attack on the promise itself: ﴿مَا وَعَدَنَا اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ إِلَّا غُرُورًا﴾. Pressure had taken them beyond fear for their own safety into thinking ill of Allah and His Messenger. According to one interpretation, ﴿الَّذِينَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ مَرَضٌ﴾ may include people whose faith is weak, not only outright hypocrites; their misgivings vary with the weakness in their hearts. The ordeal thus reveals that a person’s most dangerous loss is not comfort, but beginning to regard Allah’s promise as a deception.
