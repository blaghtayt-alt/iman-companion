## 98:1

> Those who disbelieve from the People of the Book and the polytheists were not going to desist [from disbelief][1] until there came to them clear proof,

‘Those who disbelieve from the People of the Book and the polytheists were not going to desist [from disbelief][1] until there came to them clear proof’ depicts how firmly people can cling to a familiar way of life. Mere wishes or words do not free them from it; they need clear evidence that reveals the truth. The People of the Book receive particular attention because they already possessed knowledge and inherited revelation. Yet this inheritance does not replace the need to respond to the new clear proof. Evidence does not remove the freedom to choose, but it removes the excuse and makes a person’s subsequent stance clearer.

## 98:2

> a messenger from Allah, reciting pure pages[2]

The verse identifies the clear proof as ‘a messenger from Allah, reciting pure pages[2]’. Guidance did not come as a book detached from life, but through a human messenger who recites revelation, follows it, and embodies it before people. [The Arabic verb yatlu carries the senses of reciting and following.] The bearer of the message does not invent its contents; he follows what has been revealed to him. Describing the scriptures as purified places them above the tampering and distortion that affect human speech.

## 98:3

> containing upright commandments.

‘containing upright commandments’ presents the scriptures as bearing teachings that set people upright rather than lead them astray. [The Arabic word qayyimah conveys both being upright and setting others right.] The Quran offers more than information detached from a person’s formation: it lays down principles that guide thought, worship, conduct, and relationships. These scriptures also convey the truths of earlier messages in their uncorrupted form. The clear proof therefore does not overturn the foundation of revelation; it affirms its truth, distinguishes it from later distortions, and completes guidance through the final message.

## 98:4

> Yet those who were given the Scripture did not disagree[3] except after the clear proof came to them,

The problem was not always a lack of evidence: ‘Yet those who were given the Scripture did not disagree[3] except after the clear proof came to them’. Clear evidence may arrive, only for personal desires, self-interest, and blind attachment to a group to intervene, causing division after knowledge has come. A person should therefore not assume that witnessing a more powerful sign is enough, by itself, to produce faith. Someone unwilling to submit may turn even clear evidence into material for argument.

## 98:5

> although they were commanded only to worship Allah with sincere devotion to Him, being inclined to the true faith, and to establish prayer and give zakah; that is the true religion.

The verse sums up the foundations of upright religion: ‘although they were commanded only to worship Allah with sincere devotion to Him, being inclined to the true faith, and to establish prayer and give zakah’. Sincerity directs worship, the authority to prescribe religious law, and a person’s whole orientation to Allah alone. [The Arabic word hunafa’ describes turning away from associating partners with Allah towards worshipping Him alone.] This orientation then appears in prayer, which connects the servant to the Lord, and obligatory charity, which extends the servant’s goodness to others. A claim of belonging is not enough when it is detached from these foundations, which the Quran calls ‘the true religion’.

## 98:6

> Those who disbelieve from the People of the Book and the polytheists will be in the fire of Hell, abiding therein forever. It is they who are the worst of creatures.

Once clear evidence has come, persisting in disbelief is a stance towards a light that a person has been shown and then turned away from. The Quran therefore describes such people as ‘the worst of creatures’. The harm of rejecting revelation does not necessarily remain within the individual: it can deprive that person and others of guidance that would set their lives right. A person may possess many means of sustaining life, yet by obstructing access to revelation, they obstruct the nourishment of the heart and the standard that governs how those means should be used.

## 98:7

> As for those who believe and do righteous deeds – it is they who are the best of creatures.

By contrast, ‘As for those who believe and do righteous deeds – it is they who are the best of creatures’. Here, faith does not stop at knowledge; it becomes righteous action that demonstrates that clear evidence has entered a person’s life. Being the best is not a distinction conferred by a name. It is the fruit of someone putting themselves right and making what they learn a source of good around them. Revelation thus becomes more than a text that is read: its effects can be seen.

## 98:8

> Their reward with their Lord is Gardens of Eternity under which rivers flow, abiding therein forever. Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him. This is for those who fear their Lord.

The reward culminates in something deeper than the delights of Paradise alone: ‘Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him’. Attaining Allah’s pleasure is the aim of their deeds, and they are pleased with Him for the reward He has given them. This contentment has a foundation that the believer seeks in this life: to be at peace with Allah as Lord and His guidance as the path to follow, and to accept His religion willingly and submissively. The verse then connects this station to ‘those who fear their Lord’. Reverent fear within the heart keeps knowledge from becoming arrogance and moves a person towards sincerity and action.
