## 99:1

> When the earth is shaken with a mighty quake,

The surah opens with a shaking that breaks the familiar rhythm of a life we imagine will carry on unchanged. This is not an earthquake in one part of the earth: the earth itself undergoes a mighty quake. [The Arabic زِلْزَالَهَا attributes the shaking to the earth: its own immense upheaval, before which all the earthquakes people have known seem small. The form زلزلة also conveys repeated, successive disturbance.] This will not be a brief moment people wait out before returning home as before. The opening therefore awakens the heart before the event: the stability on which you build your plans and hopes is not everlasting. A day will come when the whole setting changes at Allah’s command. In this way, the surah begins to shape the heart by shaking its assumptions, so that it reconsiders what it once regarded as secure.

## 99:2

> and the earth throws out its burdens[1],

After the earth is shaken, it brings out its burdens. One interpretation understands these burdens as the dead whom the earth has held; another includes the treasures within it to which people’s hearts have become attached, as though the earth were returning what had been entrusted to it. The Prophetic report about the emergence of treasures shows how their value in people’s eyes can change: someone who killed for wealth, severed family ties for it or stole it passes by and sees it lying there, with no one competing to possess it. 

The report itself does not specify that this occurs after resurrection. What once weighed so heavily in worldly calculations proves worthless when set against one’s final destiny. One interpretation calls these things burdens because they were a weighty trust within the earth; another relates the name to the weight they carried in people’s estimation. Either way, the ayah puts values back in order: the earth brings out what it contains, and the human being will have to account for the responsibility of their deeds.

## 99:3

> and man says[2], “What is the matter with it?”

A person sees the earth, once so familiar and stable, shaken and bringing out what lies within it, and asks: “What is the matter with it?” The question expresses real shock. They once walked upon it, pursuing their desires and plans as though the scene would never change; now the very place they inhabited behaves in an unfamiliar way. They realize that a change of such magnitude cannot be purposeless: what momentous event is all this for? The surah invites its reader to face that question now, before having to ask it there. If you understand today why the earth will change, you can change your own conduct before the answer comes and there is no turning back.

Sources: Quran 99:3 · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/99/3

## 99:4

> On that Day it will recount all its news,

The answer is that the earth itself will recount all its news. The place where deeds were performed in this world becomes a witness in the Hereafter. Footsteps their owner thought forgotten, a wronged person’s blood, hidden wealth, a spoken word, a sin committed where no one could see, and steps taken in obedience: the place has a story to tell about all of them. Its role is reversed. The earth once silently bore people and their actions; now the time has come for it to speak. Allah has kept their complete record, while they have forgotten it. Human forgetfulness does not erase an event, and concealment from people’s eyes does not mean that the place has no testimony to give.

## 99:5

> because your Lord has inspired it [to do so].

The earth does not speak of its own accord: because your Lord has inspired it [to do so]. Just as it remained silent at Allah’s command, it speaks at His command. Revelation here conveys a swift command that the earth obeys without hesitation. This also challenges the human being: the solid earth changes its condition at once when its Lord commands it, yet a person may hear the revelation they were created to follow and refuse to change. Created things await their Creator’s command, while the servant who can choose delays and puts it off. The ayah teaches that the earth’s ability to testify is not independent of Allah. The whole reckoning takes place under the care and command of your Lord, who rules over place, time and the tongue that speaks.

## 99:6

> On that Day all people will come forward in separate groups to be shown their deeds[3].

After the testimony, all people will come forward in separate groups to be shown their deeds. Their conditions differ from one person to another. The ayah presents all of them moving at once, each absorbed in their own result. This is not a medical test whose consequences end after some years, but the outcome on which an everlasting future depends. [The Arabic لِيُرَوْا means that they are to be shown their deeds.] The matter is more than a brief written result: deeds their doers had forgotten will appear, together with their effects and recompense. From Adam to the last human being, each person faces what they have done. The collective excuses behind which people hid then fall away. Saying that everyone did the same will not help, because each person sees their own deeds.

Sources: Quran 99:6 · https://quranenc.com/en/browse/english_rwwad/99/6

## 99:7

> So whoever does an atom’s weight[4] of good will see it,

The measure reaches down to an atom’s weight. Good that its doer thought insignificant is not lost. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said: “So, protect yourselves from Hellfire even with half a date.” He also said: “Do not belittle any good deed, even if it be meeting your brother with a cheerful face.” This answers anyone embarrassed to give only a little: little is not insignificant on a scale that accounts for the smallest particles. A smile, a kind word, a modest gift of charity, a step toward obedience—sincere good does not disappear. One interpretation of seeing it is that a person sees the deed itself; another is that they see its recompense. The practical lesson is the same: do good, and do not belittle what you are able to offer. Allah does not require an act to be large before He rewards it.

## 99:8

> and whoever does an atom’s weight of evil will see it.

Just as not the smallest particle of good is lost, not the smallest particle of evil disappears. The ayah overturns excuses such as ‘It was only a word’ or ‘It was a minor sin.’ A measure that accounts for the smallest particles leaves no room for dismissing them. This particularly exposes the danger of words to which a person gives no thought, and of small sins repeated until the heart grows used to them. A report from Ibn Masud (may Allah be pleased with him) illustrates this difference. 

A believer regards their sin as serious because they know whom they have disobeyed and the reckoning they will face. A wicked person dismisses it from their thoughts like a passing fly. Thus the surah moves from the earthquake to the scale: if your view of this world’s value has been shaken, your conduct must change. Do not belittle good or dismiss evil. Take care over your words, your livelihood and your time before every particle is weighed.

Sources: Sahih al-Bukhari 6308 · Ibn Masud’s report · https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6308
