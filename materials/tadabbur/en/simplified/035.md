Surah Fatir (35)
Simplified version


Quran translations: Rowwad Translation Center; Fatir 35:25: Noor International Center.
Commentary: Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim, five sessions on Surah Fatir.
The report from al-Tabarani 3718 uses the published translation by Abu Amina Elias. Other reports and historical accounts are referenced in the text.


Verse 1

All praise be to Allah, the Originator of the heavens and earth, Who made the angels messengers with two, three, or four wings. He increases in creation whatever He wills. Indeed, Allah is Most Capable of all things.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

Allah opens the surah with praise of Himself as the Originator of the heavens and earth. The word translated “Originator” belongs to a family of Arabic words associated with beginning creation without a prior model, splitting, and the beginning from which creation emerges and grows. The angels then show diversity within a single kind of creation, despite sharing their origin and function. Their different wings and the differences in rank among them are by Allah’s decree: “He increases in creation whatever He wills.” Nothing is beyond His power.


Verse 2

Whatever mercy Allah opens up for people, none can withhold it; and whatever He withholds, none can release it thereafter, for He is the All-Mighty, All-Wise.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

When Allah opens mercy for His servants, no one can close it; when He withholds something, no one can release it after Him. This attaches the heart to Allah alone. Worldly means do not independently give or withhold: they operate under the authority of the All-Mighty, who cannot be overcome, and the All-Wise, who gives and withholds in their proper place.


Verse 3

O people, remember Allah’s favor upon you. Is there any creator other than Allah who gives you provision from heaven and earth? None has the right to be worshiped except Him. How can you then be deluded?

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

Allah reminds people of the source of every blessing: no creator other than Him provides from heaven and earth. Since creation and provision are in His hands alone, directing worship or absolute attachment to another turns the truth upside down. The question therefore comes to awaken the heart: “How can you then be deluded?”


Verse 4

If they reject you, there were messengers rejected before you; it is to Allah that all matters will be returned.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

People’s rejection of the Messenger was not an isolated event; it had happened repeatedly to earlier messengers. The truth is therefore not measured by how quickly people accept it. All matters return to Allah. Those who uphold the truth must fulfil their duty and avoid making people’s rejection their measure of what is true.


Verse 5

O people, indeed Allah’s promise is true, so do not let the life of this world deceive you, nor let the Chief Deceiver [Satan] deceive you concerning Allah.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

Allah’s promise is true, however much worldly appearances may seem to contradict it. The verse therefore warns against two sources of heedlessness: the glitter of worldly life, and the deceiver who makes falsehood attractive and fills people with false hopes. Steadfastness begins when Allah’s promise carries more weight in the heart than the immediate scene.


Verse 6

Indeed, Satan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy. He only calls his followers to be among the inmates of the Blazing Fire.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

Satan is an enemy. He must not be treated as a sincere adviser, nor should someone follow his path and then expect good from him. His aim is for people to become inmates of the Blazing Fire. Knowing his enmity requires opposing his steps, not merely acknowledging in theory that he is an enemy.


Verse 7

Those who disbelieve will have a severe punishment, but those who believe and do righteous deeds will have forgiveness and a great reward.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

Different paths lead to different outcomes: severe punishment for those who disbelieve, and forgiveness and great reward for people of faith and righteous action. The difference lies in neither claims nor appearances, but in the reality of faith and the deeds it produces.


Verse 8

Is one whose evil deed is made appealing to him so he deems it good [like one who is rightly guided]? It is Allah who causes to stray whom He wills and guides whom He wills. So do not let yourself collapse in grief for them. Allah is All-Knowing of what they do.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

Making something look attractive can lead a person to regard their own evil deed as good, corrupting their inner standard. Guidance and misguidance are subject to Allah’s will. We must therefore not consume ourselves with grief over those who choose to turn away after clarification. Our duty is to convey the message and act; Allah knows what they do.


Verse 9

It is Allah Who sends the winds which stir up clouds, then We drive them to a dead land and revive therewith the earth after its death. Such will be the Resurrection.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

The sending of the winds, their stirring up clouds, and the driving of those clouds to dead land until it comes to life visibly show Allah’s power to give life after death. The scene also inspires hope: just as the means of life are brought to dead land, the means of guidance are brought to hearts. The land’s death does not prevent it from living when what gives it life arrives.


Verse 10

Whoever seeks honor, then all honor belongs to Allah alone. To Him ascend good words, and righteous deeds raise them[1]. But those who plan evil deeds will have a severe punishment, and the plans of such people will perish.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; first session, verses 1–10.

All honour belongs to Allah. It must not be sought by disobeying Him or by attaching oneself to those who do not possess it. Good words ascend to Allah, and several explanations are given of their relationship with righteous deeds: deeds confirm and raise words; or the good words of declaring Allah’s oneness and remembering Him raise deeds. An application is that supplication must be accompanied by effort and action. In all of this, outward rectitude remains joined to integrity in action. Evil plotting, however long it lasts, ends in ruin.

Notes

[1] To Allah, i.e., righteous deeds are confirmation of what is uttered by the tongue.


Verse 11

It is Allah Who created you from dust[2], then from a drop of semen, and then He made you into pairs[3]. No female conceives nor gives birth without His knowledge. None is given long life or cut short of his life except that it is in a Record[4]. That is surely easy for Allah.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

Humanity’s journey runs from dust to a drop of semen, then to pairing, pregnancy, birth and a lifespan. At every stage, people remain under Allah’s knowledge and decree. Differences in lifespans and circumstances never fall outside His record. The details of all creation that seem complicated to us are easy for Allah.

Notes

[2] i.e., your father, Adam. [3] i.e., into males and females.
 [4] i.e., the Preserved Tablet (al-Lawh al-Mahfūzh).


Verse 12

The two bodies of water are not alike: one is fresh and palatable, pleasant to drink, and the other is salty and bitter. Yet from each you eat fresh meat and extract ornaments to wear. And you see the ships traverse their way through both, so that you may seek His bounty and so that you may give thanks.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

The difference between the two bodies of water does not prevent both from benefiting people. One is fresh and sweet, the other salty and bitter; both provide food and ornaments, and ships sail through them. Diversity in Allah’s creation can bring complementary benefits. The servant should see Allah’s bounty in things’ differences as well as in their similarities.


Verse 13

He merges the night into the day and the day into the night, and subjected the sun and the moon, each running its course for an appointed term. Such is Allah, your Lord. To Him belongs the dominion, whereas those whom you invoke besides Him do not own so much as the membrane of a date stone.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

The merging of night into day and day into night, and the subjection of the sun and moon until an appointed term, all fall under one dominion. Supposed objects of worship besides Allah own not even the membrane of a date stone. The difference becomes clear between Allah’s real dominion and the means or false gods to which people attach themselves, though these possess nothing of their own.


Verse 14

If you call upon them, they cannot hear your call, and even if they were to hear, they could not respond to you. On the Day of Resurrection they will disown your associating them as partners with Allah[5]. And none can inform you like the All-Aware.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

Those invoked in worship besides Allah do not truly hear the supplication; even if they heard, they could not answer it. Then, on the Day of Resurrection, they disavow this association of partners with Him. No one tells the realities of dominion and the final outcome like the All-Aware, exalted is He. Our attachment and worship must therefore not be built on human illusions.

Notes

[5] Or your worship of them.


Verse 15

O people, it is you who are in need of Allah, whereas Allah is the Self-Sufficient, the Praiseworthy.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

People are inherently in need of Allah for their existence, continued life, provision and guidance. Allah alone is Self-Sufficient and Praiseworthy. The obedience of the obedient adds nothing to His dominion, and the disobedience of the disobedient takes nothing from it. The effect of worship returns to the servant themselves.


Verse 16

If He wills, He can eliminate you and bring in a new creation,

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

If Allah willed, He could remove all people. The continued existence of creation does not mean that He cannot replace them; it reflects His forbearance, respite and decree. Keeping this meaning in mind cuts off the feeling of self-sufficiency and helps a person see their very existence as a blessing, rather than an entitlement.


Verse 17

for that is not difficult for Allah.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, verses 11–18.

Bringing a new creation into existence is neither difficult nor burdensome for Allah. Whoever knows that they began from nonexistence, and that their continuance and departure are in Allah’s hand, will not dismiss anything as beyond His power or make human measures a limit to what He can do.


Verse 18

No bearer of burdens can bear the burden of another. If a heavily-laden soul calls for help to bear its burden, nothing of it will be carried, even if he is a close relative. You can only warn those who fear their Lord unseen[6] and establish prayer. Whoever purifies himself, he only does so for his own benefit. To Allah is the final return.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; second session, with a supporting passage from the fifth session.

On the Day of Resurrection no one carries another’s burden of sin, and every soul is occupied with its own load. Even a heavily-burdened soul calling for help will have nothing carried on its behalf, though the person called is among its closest relatives. This dispels the illusion of relying on people and lineage. Those who fear their Lord unseen and establish prayer benefit from warning. Whoever purifies themselves receives the fruit of that purification, and to Allah is the final return.

Notes

[6] Without seeing Him or when they are in private.


Verse 19

The blind and the seeing are not equal[7],

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Just as the blind and the seeing are not equal, someone who has lost the heart’s insight differs from someone who sees through revelation. The clarity of this tangible difference teaches the heart that the difference between faith and disbelief is not a minor detail that worldly adornments can erase.

Notes

[7] It refers to believers and disbelievers.


Verse 20

nor are the depths of darkness and the light[8],

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Darkness and light are not equal. The heart needs a sound innate disposition and revelation to guide it, just as the eye needs both the ability to see and light. Whoever lacks revelation lives in darkness, however many worldly tools surround them.

Notes

[8] It refers to belief and disbelief.


Verse 21

nor the shade and the scorching wind[9].

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Shade is not equal to scorching heat. Faith carries a sense of tranquillity and stability, while disbelief and bewilderment leave the heart in anxiety and turmoil. The matter is not merely information, but the path’s effect on the heart and its final outcome.

Notes

[9] It refers to Paradise and Hell.


Verse 22

Nor are the living and the dead equal. Allah makes to hear whom He wills, but you cannot make to hear those who are in the graves[10].

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

The living and the dead are not equal. A heart alive through guidance differs from a heart dead through turning away. Allah makes whom He wills hear in a way that benefits and guides them; the caller cannot create a response in anyone’s heart.

Notes

[10] It refers to believers and disbelievers.


Verse 23

You are but a warner.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

The Messenger’s task is to convey the message and warn. He cannot force guidance into people’s hearts. This frees those working for truth from carrying what is beyond them and returns them to their real responsibility: honest explanation and steadfastness.


Verse 24

We have sent you with the truth as a bearer of glad tidings and a warner. There has never been any community except that a warner came to it[11].

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

The message came with truth, giving glad tidings and warning. No community was left without a warner. Establishing the proof and showing mercy to creation are part of an enduring way; a lack of response does not prove a lack of explanation.

Notes

[11] Among all prophets and messengers, only twenty-five are mentioned in the Qur’an.


Verse 25

And if they deny you - then already have those before them denied. Their messengers came to them with clear proofs and written ordinances and with the enlightening Scripture.

Quran translation: Noor International Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Rejection by nations is not new. Before the Prophet, messengers brought clear proofs, scriptures and the illuminating Book, yet were rejected. Abundant evidence does not benefit someone whose fundamental stance is to reject the truth. Those calling to it should therefore not assume that the problem always lies in insufficient explanation.


Verse 26

Then I seized those who disbelieved. How severe was My response!

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Allah’s seizure of those who rejected after the proof had been established shows that respite does not mean neglect. Then comes: “How severe was My response!” It calls us to reassess strength and splendour in the light of the outcome, rather than the present moment.


Verse 27

Do you not see that Allah sends down rain from the sky, then We bring forth thereby fruits of various colors? And in the mountains there are various tracts of white and red, of various colors, and raven black;

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

The same water falls from the sky, and fruits of different colours grow through it. The mountains themselves contain varied white, red and black streaks. Different colours from one origin testify to the breadth of divine power. Variety is not outside Allah’s decree; it is among His signs.


Verse 28

just as humans, animals and livestock are of various colors. Only the people of knowledge among His slaves fear Allah. Indeed, Allah is All-Mighty, All-Forgiving.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Variety also appears among people, animals and livestock. Allah then explains that the fruit of true knowledge is fear of Him: “Only the people of knowledge among His slaves fear Allah.” Knowledge that does not increase a person’s reverence for Allah and fear of Him has not reached its purpose.


Verse 29

Those who recite the Book of Allah, establish prayer, and spend out of what We have provided for them, secretly and openly, hoping for a deal that will suffer no loss,

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Continuing recitation of Allah’s Book, establishing prayer and spending secretly and openly form a trade that suffers no loss. Renewed contact with the Quran prevents the heart from drying out and renews meaning and action. The Quran thus becomes a source of renewed life, rather than a recitation routine whose effects stop there.


Verse 30

so that He will reward them in full and give them more out of His bounty[14]. He is All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

Allah rewards the people of this trade in full, then gives them more from His bounty. His giving does not stop at the measure of the deed. He forgives their shortcomings and appreciates their little obedience, nurturing it for them and giving beyond the reward due for their deeds.

Notes

[14] i.e., Allah gives much in return for little.


Verse 31

The Book that We have revealed to you [O Prophet] is the truth, confirming that which came before it. Indeed, Allah is All-Aware and All-Seeing of His slaves.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, verses 19–32.

The revelation sent to the Prophet is truth, confirming the truth that preceded it. Allah knows His servants fully and sees who is suited to carry the message. Choosing the Prophet to receive revelation was not by chance, but through Allah’s perfect knowledge of who would carry this light.


Verse 32

Then We made to inherit the Book those whom We have chosen from among Our slaves[15]. But among them are some who wrong themselves, some follow a middle course, and some are foremost in good deeds with Allah’s permission. That is the great bounty.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; third session, continued at the beginning of the fourth session.

Allah made a chosen community inherit the Book, yet people differ in receiving it: one wrongs themselves, another follows a middle course, and another is foremost in good deeds by Allah’s permission. Interpretations of these groups differ, but the shared lesson is that closeness to the Quran has more than one degree. Being foremost is itself Allah’s enabling, leaving no room for conceit.

Notes

[15] i.e., the Prophet Muhammad’s (ﷺ) followers.


Verse 33

They will enter Gardens of Eternity; they will be adorned therein with bracelets of gold and pearls, and their garments will be of silk.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

The Gardens of Eternity, with their bracelets, gold, pearls and silk, fully compensate believers for the worldly adornment they left when occupied with supporting the religion and obedience. What is left for Allah does not go to waste. The servant receives in its place what lasts longer and is more complete.


Verse 34

And they will say, “All praise be to Allah Who has taken away from us all sorrow. Indeed, our Lord is All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative,

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

The first words of the people of Paradise are praise to Allah who has taken their sorrow away. Behind their longing for Paradise lay fear and a burden in the heart, including fear of the Fire and the hardship they met along the way. The lifting of sorrow when they enter Paradise matters more to them than the adornment they see.


Verse 35

He Who has caused us to settle – out of His grace – in the everlasting home, wherein no weariness or fatigue will touch us.”

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

The everlasting home is a place of stability after the movement and fatigue of this world. Neither weariness nor exhaustion touches its people. Those who lived for the religion, gave, travelled and toiled find there a settled home interrupted by neither journey nor hardship.


Verse 36

As for those who disbelieve, there will be the Fire of Hell; neither will they be sentenced to death, so that they may die, nor will its punishment be lightened for them. This is how We recompense every disbeliever.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

For those who disbelieve there is a Fire in which they are neither put to death nor given relief from its punishment. Here appears the final difference that worldly adornment had concealed: the ultimate outcome is not measured by temporary displays of power.


Verse 37

They will be screaming therein, “Our Lord, take us out; we will do righteous deeds, unlike what we used to do.” [They will be told], “Did We not give you long enough lives to take heed for anyone who wished to take heed? And the warner did come to you. So taste! The wrongdoers will have no helper.”

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

The people of the Fire cry out, asking to return and do righteous deeds. They are reminded that they had a lifetime long enough to take heed and that the warner had come. The problem was not a missing opportunity, but one they wasted. After the case against them is fully established, only tasting the consequence remains.


Verse 38

Allah is the Knower of the unseen in the heavens and earth. He is All-Knowing of what is in the hearts.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

Allah knows the unseen of the heavens and earth and all that is within hearts. No faith, hypocrisy or hidden motive escapes Him. His measure reaches beyond outward appearance to what has settled in the heart.


Verse 39

It is He Who has made you successors on earth. Whoever disbelieves will bear the burden of his disbelief. Their disbelief only increases the disbelievers in contempt before their Lord, and their disbelief only increases the disbelievers in loss.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

Allah made people successors on earth and charged them with responsibility. Whoever disbelieves bears the consequence of that disbelief. Disbelief increases its doer in abhorrence before Allah and in loss, however successful or advanced they appear in this world. The divine measure does not change with passing adornment.


Verse 40

Say, “Tell me about your associate-gods whom you supplicate besides Allah. Show me what they created on earth, or do they have a share in the heavens? Or have We given them a Book so they have clear proof? No, the wrongdoers promise one another nothing but delusion[16].”

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, verses 33–41.

The polytheists are asked about their partners: what have they created on earth? Do they have a share in the heavens? Do they possess a Book giving them clear evidence? There is neither rational proof nor revealed authority, only promises exchanged by wrongdoers who deceive one another. Many followers do not make a proof.

Notes

[16] By giving their followers deceptive hopes that their false deities will intercede for them with Allah.


Verse 41

Allah keeps the heavens and earth from vanishing. If they were to vanish, there is none to sustain them other than Him. He is Most Forbearing, All-Forgiving.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fourth session, continued at the opening of the fifth.

Allah sustains the heavens and earth so that they do not vanish. If they vanished, none could sustain them after Him. The Quran widens the human horizon from small problems to the order of the whole universe. The world’s very continuance depends on Allah’s preservation, and despite people’s disobedience He remains Forbearing and Forgiving.


Verse 42

They swore by Allah their most solemn oaths that if a warner came to them, they would surely be more guided than any other community. But when a warner came to them, it only added to their aversion –

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fifth session, verses 42–45.

They swore the strongest oaths that if a warner came, they would be better guided than earlier communities. When he came, their aversion only grew. Putting guidance off until conditions improve or an ideal opportunity arrives is exposed by the test. Someone who truly wants truth begins with what they can do before circumstances change.


Verse 43

due to their arrogance in the land and plotting evil, but evil plotting only backfires on its own people. Are they awaiting anything other than the fate of the earlier peoples? You will find no change in Allah’s way[17], and you will find no alteration in Allah’s way[18].

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fifth session, verses 42–45.

Behind their aversion lay arrogance in the land and evil plotting. In the end, evil plotting surrounds only its own people. Allah’s way is neither changed nor diverted. No one can put an appointed outcome where it does not belong, or bypass His ways through wishes or power alone.

Notes

[17] In punishing those who deny the prophets.
 [18] In transferring punishment to others in place of them.


Verse 44

Have they not traveled in the land to see what was the end of those who came before them? They were more powerful than them. Allah is not such that something can escape Him in the heavens or on earth. He is indeed All-Knowing, Most Capable.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fifth session, verses 42–45.

Travel through the land reveals that stronger nations existed and then met their end. Strength did not put them beyond Allah’s power. Nothing in the heavens or earth can defeat Him, for He is All-Knowing and All-Powerful. Perfect knowledge joined with perfect power means that neither inability nor ignorance obstructs His will.


Verse 45

If Allah were to take people to task for what they did, He would not leave a single living being on earth, but He gives them respite for an appointed term. When their appointed time comes, then Allah is All-Seeing of His slaves.

Quran translation: Rowwad Translation Center.
Source: Commentary on Surah Fatir — Dr. Ahmad Abd al-Munʿim; fifth session, verses 42–45.

If Allah immediately took people to task for everything they earned, He would leave no living creature on earth. But He gives them time until an appointed term. Delayed punishment means neither unawareness nor a broken promise. Truth may become manifest after both oppressor and victim have died, and the reckoning will be fulfilled in the Hereafter. Allah sees His servants, and nothing is lost with Him.
