# Sayings of Ibn al-Qayyim · English

The saying and its explanation are separate. Source records and required notices accompany each item; these are not complete editions of the cited books.

For iq-11, the wording is reported in the cited book; its personal authorship remains unresolved. The Arabic explanation is a separate human-supplied paragraph.

## iq-1

Ibn al-Qayyim

«This world is a passage, and the Hereafter is home; what we truly seek belongs in our homeland.»

Al-Fawāʾid

This world is a stage on a journey, not the final destination. Wealth and pleasures are used to meet one’s needs, while one’s efforts are directed towards what will benefit a person after death, just as a traveller does not make the road a permanent home.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-2

Ibn al-Qayyim

«When hearts turn away from the banquets of this world, they sit at the banquets of the Hereafter among those invited; when they settle for this world's banquets, they miss the others.»

Al-Fawāʾid

The tables of this world represent pleasures that may distract a person from Allah; the tables of the Hereafter represent what He has prepared for those who turn to Him. Attachment to the former can cause the latter to be forgotten.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-3

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever's heart is filled with love and reverence for God does not consider what they gave up of this world for His sake worthy of being called a sacrifice.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

A person who gives up a pleasure or advantage to seek Allah’s pleasure does not regard the sacrifice as something immense. Closeness to Allah is worth far more than whatever worldly benefit was relinquished.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.al-eman.com/الكتب/مدارج%20السالكين%20بين%20منازل%20إياك%20نعبد%20وإياك%20نستعين%20(نسخة%20منقحة)/i915&n41&p1)

## iq-4

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Power, status, wealth and even spiritual states are given to the righteous and the wicked, to believers and unbelievers alike.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Wealth and influence can belong to people who are not righteous. Worldly success alone therefore does not establish that Allah is pleased with the person who enjoys it.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/46/فصل-أقسام-الناس-في-العبادة-والاستعانة)

## iq-5

Ibn al-Qayyim

«People differ in their aspirations, not in their outward appearances.»

Al-Fawāʾid

True distinction lies not in appearance or fame, but in the nobility of a person’s aims and the effort devoted to doing good.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-6

Ibn al-Qayyim

«When God wishes good for a servant, He makes them acknowledge their own sins, refrain from dwelling on others’ sins, give generously from what they have, avoid coveting others’ possessions, and patiently bear harm from others.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Acknowledging one’s own faults is the beginning of self-reform, unlike preoccupation with the faults of others. This goes together with being generous to others from what one has, without coveting what they possess.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-7

Ibn al-Qayyim

«A deed without sincerity and without following prophetic guidance is like a traveler filling a bag with sand: it weighs them down but brings no benefit.»

Al-Fawāʾid

A large number of deeds is of no benefit if their purpose is to win people’s praise or if they run contrary to the Prophet’s ﷺ guidance. Their value depends on sincerity and on following his example.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-8

Ibn al-Qayyim

«The most beneficial deed is one in which sincerity makes you forget people's attention, and awareness of God's favor makes you forget your own merit; you focus neither on yourself nor on others.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Sincerity frees good deeds from the expectation of people’s praise, while recognising Allah’s favour prevents self-admiration. The very ability to do good is itself a blessing from Him.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-9

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever knows their own self becomes occupied with correcting it rather than finding fault with others.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Awareness of one’s own faults and of what needs to be put right leaves less room for scrutinising other people’s shortcomings and passing judgment on them.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-10

Ibn al-Qayyim

«God has spared us competition over this world's fleeting gains by inviting us to compete for the Hereafter and what He has prepared there.»

Ighāthat al-Lahfān

Beneficial competition concerns deeds whose good endures into the Hereafter, such as seeking knowledge and giving charity, rather than comparing one’s wealth and standing with those of others.

[Ighāthat al-Lahfān](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/إغاثة_اللهفان/الباب_الثالث_عشر/20)

## iq-11

Ibn al-Qayyim

«The more someone knows God and His attributes, the sounder and stronger their reliance on Him becomes.»

Quoted in · Madārij as-Sālikīn

The more a person knows Allah, His names and His attributes, and the more certain they become of His power, wisdom, mercy and His management of all affairs, the stronger their reliance on Him becomes and the more reassurance their heart finds in His choice. This is because they know that their affairs are in the hands of a Lord who is all-knowing, wise and merciful, and for whom nothing is beyond His power.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/267/حقيقة-التوكل-مركبة-في-أمور)

## iq-12

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Entrusting matters to God means placing all your affairs before Him willingly and by choice, not reluctantly or only out of necessity.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Entrusting one’s affairs to Allah means choosing from the outset to place them in His care and seek His help, rather than turning to Him only after other attempts have failed. It combines earnest effort with leaving to Allah those outcomes that lie beyond human control.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/273/فصل-الدرجة-السابعة-التفويض)

## iq-13

Ibn al-Qayyim

The fruit of true reliance: «When a person genuinely trusts God, they accept what He chooses for them.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

True reliance on Allah is shown by continuing to trust Him after making every effort, even when the hoped-for outcome does not occur. Such trust is not conditional on one’s wishes being fulfilled.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/274/فصل-ثمرة-التوكل)

## iq-14

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Reliance on God is a state of the heart born of knowing Him and believing that He alone creates and governs, brings harm and benefit, and gives and withholds.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Taking practical steps, such as working or seeking medical treatment, goes together with recognising that the results are not in human hands alone and seeking Allah’s help in attaining them.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/46/فصل-أقسام-الناس-في-العبادة-والاستعانة)

## iq-15

Ibn al-Qayyim

«A servant's love for their Lord surpasses every love that can be measured; other loves cannot compare with it.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Love for one’s family and for the things of this world is natural. When a desire conflicts with Allah’s command, however, obedience to Him takes precedence.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/482/%3Fidfrom=&idto=&start=)

## iq-16

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Hearts are naturally inclined to love those who have been kind to them.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Reflecting on familiar blessings, such as health, security and the love of family, renews awareness of their value. Recognising Allah’s kindness increases love for Him in the heart.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/489/الدرجة-الأولى-محبة-تقطع-الوساوس)

## iq-17

Ibn al-Qayyim

A sincere lover finds companionship with God among people just as they find it in solitude.

Al-Fawāʾid (paraphrased)

The comfort found in closeness to Allah is not confined to solitude. It also extends to remembering and obeying Him at work and among other people.

[Al-Fawāʾid (paraphrased)](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-18

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Complete delight, joy, a good life and bliss are found in knowing God, affirming His oneness, feeling close to Him, longing to meet Him and directing the heart toward Him.»

Risālat Ibn al-Qayyim ilā Aḥad Ikhwānih

The joy brought by wealth or success may fade. Knowing Allah and finding closeness to Him through worship give life a meaning that does not depend on such gains.

[Risālat Ibn al-Qayyim ilā Aḥad Ikhwānih](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/rsalt-abn-alqim-ila-ahd-ikhwanh.html)

## iq-19

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Repentance for a sin must be undertaken immediately; it must not be delayed.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Repentance is not postponed until a convenient time. It entails abandoning the sin, regretting it and resolving not to return to it, as well as restoring the rights of others where required.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/115/فصل-التوبة-من-الذنب-فرض)

## iq-20

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Repentance is among the greatest good deeds.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Repentance is not a reason for self-admiration, but a blessing that calls for gratitude to Allah for enabling the return to Him, along with continued efforts to remedy one’s shortcomings.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/92/فصل-التوبة-من-التوبة)

## iq-21

Ibn al-Qayyim

«The heart falls ill as the body does: repentance and restraint heal it; it rusts as a mirror does, and remembrance of God polishes it.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Just as an ill person needs medicine and must avoid what worsens the illness, the heart needs repentance, remembrance of Allah and distance from whatever draws it back into sin.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-22

Ibn al-Qayyim

«If you want to benefit from the Qur'an, give it your full attention while reciting or listening to it.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Benefiting from the Quran requires attention to its meanings and an understanding of what Allah asks of His servant. Simply making the completion of pages one’s sole concern does not achieve this.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-23

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever knows the value of what they seek finds it easier to bear what they must give for it.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Remembering the hope of Allah’s pleasure and reward makes it easier to persevere in obedience, even when doing so requires effort and patience.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/229/الدرجة-الأولى-رجاء-يبعث-العامل-على-الاجتهاد)

## iq-24

Ibn al-Qayyim

«A person's blessing lies in teaching good wherever they go and offering sincere advice to those they meet.»

Risālat Ibn al-Qayyim ilā Aḥad Ikhwānih

A person’s presence among others is a source of blessing through sharing beneficial knowledge and offering counsel to those who need it, rather than keeping all concern focused on oneself.

[Risālat Ibn al-Qayyim ilā Aḥad Ikhwānih](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/rsalt-abn-alqim-ila-ahd-ikhwanh.html)

## iq-25

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Provision and lifespan are paired and guaranteed: as long as one's appointed time remains, provision will come.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Earning a livelihood does not require allowing fear of the future to lead a person into what is forbidden. Whatever provision reaches a person does so by Allah’s decree.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-26

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever seeks a livelihood with moderation finds relief from the misery and worries of this world.»

Al-Fawāʾid

A sound approach to earning a livelihood combines diligence with lawful means, without sacrificing health and peace of mind to an endless pursuit of more money.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-27

Ibn al-Qayyim

«When God, in His wisdom, closes one path of provision to you, He, in His mercy, opens another that is better for you.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Losing a job or missing an opportunity does not mean that all provision has come to an end. Allah may open another way that had not been anticipated.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-28

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever wants a pure heart should prefer God over their desires.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Giving up a forbidden desire to seek Allah’s pleasure protects the heart from attachment to what harms it. What matters is putting obedience to Him before something He has prohibited, even when the self is drawn to it.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-29

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Hearts attached to desires are kept from God in proportion to that attachment.»

Al-Fawāʾid

The problem is not the existence of desire itself, but its domination of the heart to the point that it consumes one’s time and draws one away from prayer and remembrance of Allah.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-30

Ibn al-Qayyim

«The soul sometimes desires what harms it because it does not know the harm, sometimes because its intentions are corrupt, and sometimes because of both.»

Ighāthat al-Lahfān

A person may fall into something harmful through ignorance of its consequences, or through weakness in resisting a desire despite knowing the harm. Both knowledge and self-discipline are therefore needed.

[Ighāthat al-Lahfān](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/إغاثة_اللهفان/الباب_الثالث_عشر/23)

## iq-31

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever makes their heart's home with their Lord finds calm and rest; whoever sends it wandering among people becomes unsettled and anxious.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Making one’s worth depend on other people’s opinions turns changes in their praise and attention into a source of anxiety. Turning the heart towards Allah frees it from making their approval the foundation of its peace.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-32

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Longing for God and meeting Him is a breeze upon the heart that cools the heat of this world.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Remembering the meeting with Allah and hoping for what He has prepared broaden a person’s perspective when life’s worries become heavy. Hope is no longer confined by the distress of the present moment.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-33

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Being content with God's decree does not mean feeling no pain or hardship; it means not objecting to His judgment or resenting it.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Grief and pain in the face of hardship do not contradict acceptance of Allah’s decree. Acceptance means maintaining trust in Him without objecting to Him because of what has befallen His servant.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/305/فصل-شرط-الرضا)

## iq-34

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Strive to have one overriding concern, and let that concern be God alone.»

Risālat Ibn al-Qayyim ilā Aḥad Ikhwānih

Making Allah’s pleasure one’s central aim helps order life’s priorities, rather than leaving them scattered between other people’s expectations and the pursuit of wealth and standing.

[Risālat Ibn al-Qayyim ilā Aḥad Ikhwānih](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/rsalt-abn-alqim-ila-ahd-ikhwanh.html)

## iq-35

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Whoever glimpses the sweetness of well-being finds the bitterness of patience easier to bear.»

Al-Fawāʾid

Remembering the peace and well-being that follow overcoming a sin or harmful habit makes the struggle to resist it and patiently refrain from it easier to bear.

[Al-Fawāʾid](https://iifa-aifi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/18.الفوائد-لابن-القيم.pdf)

## iq-36

Ibn al-Qayyim

«Complaining to God Almighty does not contradict patience.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Weeping, confiding one’s pain to Allah and asking Him to lift an affliction do not contradict patience, provided they do not involve objection to Him.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/298/?idfrom=&idto=&start=)

## iq-37

Ibn al-Qayyim

«There is patience through God, patience for God, and patience with God.»

Madārij as-Sālikīn

Patience through Allah means seeking from Him the strength to be patient; patience for Allah means making His pleasure its aim; and patience with Allah means remaining obedient to Him however circumstances change.

[Madārij as-Sālikīn](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/298/?idfrom=&idto=&start=)
