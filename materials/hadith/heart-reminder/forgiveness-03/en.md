# The foremost prayer for forgiveness

Published passages

Shaddad ibn Aws reported this saying of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him).

The foremost form

The most superior way of asking for forgiveness from Allah is:

Prayer

O Allah, You are my Lord, there is none worthy of worship except You. You have created me, and I am Your servant, and I am faithful to Your covenant and promise as much as I can. I seek refuge in You from the evil of what I have done. I acknowledge Your blessings upon me, and I admit my sins. So forgive me, for none forgives sins except You.

Promise and its conditions

If somebody recites it during the day with firm faith in it, and dies on the same day before the evening, he will be from the people of Paradise; and if somebody recites it at night with firm faith in it, and dies before the morning, he will be from the people of Paradise.

Sahih al-Bukhari

Explanation of the meaning

There are different ways of asking Allah for forgiveness, and some are better than others. This is the foremost prayer for forgiveness, so the servant should make a point of saying it with an understanding of its meaning. It begins by acknowledging that Allah alone deserves worship, that He created us and that we are His servants.

The covenant concerns faith in Allah, worshipping Him alone and obeying Him as far as one is able; the promise is Allah’s promise to those who remain faithful to Him. The servant affirms that covenant and trusts His promise while admitting the limits of human ability. However much we worship, we cannot fully discharge all that Allah commands or thank Him adequately for His blessings.

The servant seeks refuge in Allah from the evil of his own deeds, willingly acknowledges Allah’s blessings and admits his own sin and disobedience. Having approached Allah through these acknowledgments, he asks Him to forgive him, cover his sins and protect him from their evil through His pardon, favour and mercy. Allah alone forgives sins.

The promise includes both conviction and the stated times of death: whoever says the prayer during the day with firm conviction and dies that same day before evening will be among the people of Paradise; whoever says it at night with firm conviction and dies before morning will also be among them. This conviction includes attending to the meaning and believing it. The source commentary places the prayer among morning and night remembrances, describing the early part of the day from sunrise to noon and the night from sunset to dawn. Those commentary details remain distinct from the wording of the report.
