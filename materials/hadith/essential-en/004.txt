Steadfastness in religion and recognizing a heart that has strayed

Al-Nawwas ibn Sam‘an al-Kilabi, may Allah be pleased with him, reported: "I heard the Messenger of Allah say: 'There is no heart that is not between two of the Fingers of the Most Merciful. If He wills, He guides it and if He wills, He sends it astray.' The Messenger of Allah used to say: 'O You Who makes hearts steadfast make our hearts steadfast in adhering to Your religion.' And he said: 'The Scale is in the Hand of the Most Merciful; He will cause some peoples to rise and others to fall until the day of Resurrection.'"

Ibn Majah, 199; authentic.

The believer asks for a steadfast heart because remaining rightly guided is beyond a person’s own power without Allah’s protection. Allah may keep a heart upright upon the truth; a heart may also deviate when it turns away from guidance. The basic meaning of the Arabic word zaygh is an inclination or turning aside, as when the sun inclines. Umm Salamah used to hear the Prophet ﷺ frequently ask Allah for steadfastness. When she asked him about hearts changing, he explained that the hearts of the children of Adam are between two of Allah’s fingers. He then asked his Lord not to let hearts deviate after guiding them, and to grant them mercy from Himself. (Ahmad, 26576.)

Alongside constant supplication, a servant needs to recognize the states of hearts that have strayed. This helps a person appreciate the blessing of being spared, thank Allah for what He has protected them from, and beware of the causes of corruption.

The swiftness of these changes is illustrated in a report that describes the human heart as turning even more vigorously than a pot at a full boil (Ahmad, 23816), and another that compares it to a feather tossed by winds in an open wilderness (Ibn Majah, 88). The feather’s lightness and the absence of anything to block the wind portray the succession of influences upon the heart: whenever the wind changes direction, the feather turns over. The singular Arabic word for wind can denote the whole kind, so it does not contradict the wording that uses the plural, winds.

The Arabic name for the heart, qalb, is connected with this turning and changing. Thoughts and desires pass through it, and knowledge enables a person to distinguish between them: hold on to what is good and resist what is evil. The Quran describes the heart’s sickness in several ways, each revealing an aspect of its loss of guidance. One is blindness: “Have they not traveled through the land so that their hearts might understand and their ears might listen? It is not the eyes that turn blind, but it is the hearts within the breasts that turn blind.” (Quran 22:46) This does not deny physical blindness, which the Quran itself recognizes: “There is no blame on a blind person, nor on the lame, nor on the sick[1], nor on yourselves[2] if you eat from your houses or from your fathers’ houses or your mothers’ houses or your brothers’ houses or your sisters’ houses or your paternal uncles’ houses, or your paternal aunts’ houses or your maternal uncles’ houses or your maternal aunts’ houses or from the houses the keys of which are in your possession, or your friends’ houses. There is no blame on you if you eat together or separately. But when you enter houses, greet one another with greetings of peace from Allah, which is blessed and delightful. This is how Allah makes the verses clear to you, so that you may understand.” (Quran 24:61) It also says: “He frowned and turned away,” (Quran 80:1) “when the blind man came to him[1].” (Quran 80:2)

The point is that the more harmful blindness is the inability to see the truth. Other reports similarly emphasize the meaning most deserving of a name. Concerning wealth: “Richness is not the abundance of wealth. Rather, true richness is self-sufficiency.” Concerning strength: “The strong man is not the one who can overpower others; rather, the strong man is the one who controls himself when he is angry.” Concerning need: “The needy is not the one who can be turned away with a date-fruit or two, or a morsel or two. But, the needy is the one who abstains from begging.” The needy person most deserving of being sought out is one who lacks enough to live on, yet whose condition goes unnoticed, so that no one gives them charity. Attention should not be confined to someone who asks and is sent away with a morsel or two. (Al-Bukhari, 6446, 6114 and 1479; Muslim, 1051, 2609 and 1039.)

Expressions of restriction also occur in particular contexts, as in the reports, “Usury is only in deferred payment” (Muslim, 1596; meaning translated from Arabic), and: “It is water (seminal discharge) that makes water (ritual bath) obligatory.” (Muslim, 343.) Here the purpose is to understand how strongly this style emphasizes a meaning within its context, rather than to derive rulings on those matters from the examples alone.

Another description is that of locks upon hearts: “Do they not reflect upon the Qur’an, or are there locks upon their hearts?” (Quran 47:24) A locked door prevents access to what lies beyond it until its lock is removed. Sealing and stamping convey a covering secured so firmly that guidance cannot enter. The Arabic term tab‘ has the more particular sense of that condition becoming an ingrained disposition: “Allah has sealed their hearts and their hearing, and their sight is covered[1]; and for them there will be a great punishment.” (Quran 2:7) “These are the ones whose hearts, hearing and sight are sealed up by Allah[1]; it is they who are the heedless.” (Quran 16:108)

Akinnah are coverings that obstruct understanding; waqr is a heaviness that prevents beneficial hearing; and a barrier keeps a person from perceiving the truth. These descriptions come together in their words: “They say, “Our hearts are covered against what you are calling us to; there is deafness in our ears, and there is a barrier between us and you. So carry on [in your way], we will carry on [in our way].”” (Quran 41:5) Ibn ‘Abbas compared the coverings to a case for arrows, and Mujahid compared them to a quiver: the image is of a veiled heart that does not understand what is said to it.

The Quran explains how this leads to turning away from the signs and recoiling when Allah alone is mentioned: “There are some among them who listen to you[1], but We have placed covers on their hearts so that they do not understand it, and deafness in their ears. Even if they were to see every sign, they would still not believe in them. When they come to you arguing, the disbelievers say, “This is nothing but ancient fables.”” (Quran 6:25) “When you recite the Qur’an, We place between you and those who do not believe in the Hereafter an invisible barrier[1].” (Quran 17:45) “We have placed covers on their hearts so that they do not understand it, and deafness in their ears[1]. When you mention your Lord alone in the Qur’an, they turn their backs in aversion.” (Quran 17:46) “Who could be more wrong than one who is reminded of the verses of his Lord, but he turns away from them and forgets what his hands have sent forth? We have placed veils on their hearts so that they cannot understand it, and deafness in their ears. Even if you call them to guidance, they will never be guided.” (Quran 18:57)

Allah also says: “those whose eyes were blind to My signs[1] and they were unable to hear [the truth][2].” (Quran 18:101) This includes eyes being veiled from the proofs of His oneness and the wonders of His power described in the revelation, as well as the heart’s insight being veiled from understanding the Quran and following its guidance. The barrier begins in the heart and its effect then reaches the sight.

Their claim that their hearts were covered was no excuse that they had been created incapable of understanding and then required to understand. Allah answered their claim: “They say, “Our hearts are wrapped.” Nay, Allah has cursed them for their disbelief, little is it that they believe.” (Quran 2:88) He also says: “[They were cursed] for breaking their covenant, rejecting the signs of Allah, killing the prophets unjustly, and for their saying, “Our hearts are wrapped up”. Rather, it is Allah Who has sealed them for their disbelief; so none of them will believe except a few –” (Quran 4:155) The sealing was a consequence of disbelief they had chosen.

Among the thickest coverings is the rust formed by repeated sins: “No indeed! Their hearts have been stained by what they used to commit[1]!” (Quran 83:14) A hadith explains that a sin leaves a black mark on the heart. If the person stops, seeks forgiveness and repents, the heart is polished clean. If the person returns to sin, the mark grows until it covers the heart; this is the rust described in the verse. (Al-Tirmidhi, 3334.) Sin building upon sin shows how the heart gradually loses its sensitivity; its corruption does not arise without a cause on the person’s part.

Other descriptions are deafness, muteness and blindness: “Deaf, dumb, and blind, so they will not return [to the straight path].” (Quran 2:18) “These are the ones whom Allah has cursed and has made them deaf and blinded their sight.” (Quran 47:23) The physical faculties may be present while failing to perform their purpose of accepting the truth: “We have surely created for Hell many jinn and humans. They have hearts with which they do not understand, eyes with which they do not see, and ears with which they do not hear[1]. They are like cattle[2], rather, they are more astray. It is they who are the heedless.” (Quran 7:179) Muteness can affect the tongue; more serious still is muteness within the heart, when it neither understands the truth nor moves the tongue to speak it.

Hearing, sight and the heart are the avenues of knowledge. If these are blocked, having abundant faculties is of no benefit: “We had given them the power to an extent that We have not given you [O Makkans], and We gave them[1] hearing, sight, and hearts. But neither their hearing, nor their sight, nor their hearts availed them in the least, for they used to reject the signs of Allah. And they were overwhelmed by what they used to ridicule.” (Quran 46:26) The image of a distant call portrays how far the message is from their hearts, like someone who cannot hear or understand because of the distance: “If We had made it a non-Arabic Qur’an, they would have said, “If only its verses were made clear? What! A non-Arabic [book] and an Arab [messenger]?” Say, “It is a guidance and healing for those who believe. As for those who do not believe, there is deafness in their ears and they are blind to [understand] it – it is as if they are being called from a far-off place[1].”” (Quran 41:44) Likewise, the covering over the sight reflects what is within the heart: “Have you seen the one who took his whims as his god, and Allah caused him to stray despite having knowledge[1], and sealed up his hearing and heart, and put a cover over his sight? Who can guide him after Allah? Will you not then take heed?” (Quran 45:23) The eye is a mirror of the good or evil that has settled there.

Another condition is being turned from the right path through falsehood being made attractive, as happened to Pharaoh: “the pathways to the heavens so that I have a look at the God of Moses, for I think he is a liar.” This is how Pharaoh’s evil deeds were made appealing to him and he was hindered from the [right] way, and the plan of Pharaoh led only to ruin.” (Quran 40:37)

Another is a heart being hardened so that it does not soften to faith, as in Moses’ supplication against Pharaoh and his chiefs: “Moses said, “Our Lord, You have given Pharaoh and his chiefs splendor and wealth in the life of this world. Our Lord, they may lead people astray from Your way. Our Lord, destroy their wealth and harden their hearts[1], so that they may not believe until they see the painful punishment.”” (Quran 10:88) “Allah said [to Moses and Aaron], “Your prayer is answered, so be steadfast on the straight path, and do not follow the way of those who do not know.”” (Quran 10:89) There is also being turned away from accepting the Quran. Allah says of people who turned away when a surah was revealed: “Whenever a Chapter is revealed, they look at one another [saying], “Is anyone watching you?” Then they slip away[1]. Allah has turned their hearts away because they are a people who do not understand.” (Quran 9:127) He first mentions their turning away and then its consequence. A heart is sound when it has both sound understanding and sound intent; they had lost both.

The cause of deviation is summed up in this verse: “And [remember] when Moses said to his people, “O my people, why do you hurt me when you know that I am a messenger of Allah to you?” So when they chose to deviate, Allah caused their hearts to deviate, for Allah does not guide the rebellious people.” (Quran 61:5) In contrast, the people of faith ask: ““Our Lord, do not let our hearts deviate[1] after You have guided us, and grant us Your mercy; for You are the Munificent Bestower.” (Quran 3:8)

Losing discernment and love of the truth ultimately brings about the heart’s death. Its owner becomes like one who cannot hear: “You cannot make the dead[1] hear, nor can you make the deaf hear the call when they turn their backs retreating.” (Quran 27:80) “Nor are the living and the dead equal. Allah makes to hear whom He wills, but you cannot make to hear those who are in the graves[1].” (Quran 35:22) The heart lives, however, by knowing the truth, accepting it and preferring it: “Is the one who was dead and We gave him life [by faith] and gave him a light with which he walks among people like the one in darkness from which he can never escape[1]? This is how the deeds of the disbelievers have been made appealing to them.” (Quran 6:122) “so that he may warn those who are truly alive[1] and establish the argument against the disbelievers.” (Quran 36:70)

Hearts are not all alike in steadfastness and hesitation. One heart has been purified through mindfulness of Allah, so good thoughts follow one another within it. Another is filled with desire and becomes at home with evil thoughts, until Satan’s sway expands and the light of certainty grows weak. A third is pulled between thoughts arising from desire and thoughts arising from faith, inclining now to one and now to the other.

Faith nourishes steadfastness: “Allah keeps those who believe steadfast with the firm Word [of faith] in the life of this world[1] and in the Hereafter. But Allah causes to stray the wrongdoers[2]; Allah does whatever He wills.” (Quran 14:27) The opposite is the hypocrites’ wavering: “wavering in between, neither belonging to these [believers], nor to those [disbelievers][1]. Whomsoever Allah causes to stray, you will never find a way [to guidance] for him.” (Quran 4:143) There is also the disturbing doubt through which disbelievers are barred from what they desire: “A barrier will be placed between them and what they desire[1], as was done with the likes of them before, for they were indeed in a disquieting doubt.” (Quran 34:54)

When desire takes control of a person, it blocks beneficial hearing and reasoning: “Have you [O Prophet] seen the one who takes his desire as his god, will you then be a keeper over him?” (Quran 25:43) “Do you think that most of them hear or understand?[1] They are just like cattle[2], rather they are further astray from the right way.” (Quran 25:44)

The Quran points to several means of being made steadfast. They include hearts being strengthened and believers being supported when meeting an enemy. The following verses describe that support during the battle of Badr, including Allah’s address to the angels: “[Remember] when He caused drowsiness to overcome you as an assurance from Him, and He sent down rain from the sky to purify you and to remove Satan’s whispers from you, and to strengthen your hearts and to steady your footsteps.” (Quran 8:11) “[Remember] when your Lord inspired the angels, “I am with you[1], so make firm the feet of those who believe. I will cast terror into the hearts of those who disbelieve. So strike their necks and strike all their limbs[2].”” (Quran 8:12) Allah also says: “O you who believe, when you encounter a group [in battle], stand firm and remember Allah much, so that you may triumph[1].” (Quran 8:45)

The accounts of the messengers strengthen the heart: “We relate to you [O Prophet] the stories of the messengers to strengthen your heart. And there has come to you in this the truth, an admonition and a reminder to the believers.” (Quran 11:120) So do the revelation of the Quran and its successive descent: “Those who disbelieve say, “Why was the Qur’an not sent down to him all at once?” [We sent it] in this way so that We may strengthen your heart thereby, and We have revealed it in a gradual manner.” (Quran 25:32) “Say, “This has been brought down by the Holy Spirit[1] from your Lord with the truth, to strengthen those who believe, and as guidance and glad tidings to the Muslims.”” (Quran 16:102) And remembrance of Allah brings tranquility to hearts: “those who believe and whose hearts find tranquility in the remembrance of Allah, for indeed in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find tranquility.” (Quran 13:28)

Sound knowledge alone is not enough if a person lacks patience. Insight needs patience alongside it, and Allah brings the two together: “We made some of them leaders[1], guiding by Our command, because they observed patience and firmly believed in Our signs.” (Quran 32:24)

One way to act upon this is to resist a passing thought before it takes hold. Ibn al-Qayyim said—meaning translated from Arabic: “Repel the passing thought. If you do not, it becomes a thought you dwell upon. Repel that thought. If you do not, it becomes a desire; fight it. If you do not, it becomes a firm resolve and intention. If you do not resist it, it becomes an action. If you do not counter that action with its opposite, it becomes a habit, and it will be difficult for you to move away from it.” (Al-Fawa’id, p. 31.)

This goes together with avoiding misleading doubts, remembering Allah often, and ceasing to rely on oneself or one’s deeds. The more complete a servant’s faith becomes, the more clearly the servant recognizes the need for their Lord. One transmitted supplication says—meaning translated from Arabic: “O Ever-Living, O Sustainer of all, I seek help through Your mercy. Set all my affairs right for me, and do not leave me to myself even for the blink of an eye.” (Al-Nasa’i, Al-Sunan al-Kubra, 10330.)

Obedience itself is a means of being made steadfast: “If We were to command them, “Kill yourselves”, or “Leave your homes”, they would not do it except for a few of them. But if they did what they were instructed, it would surely be better for them and more reassuring[1],” (Quran 4:66) This is followed by the promise of reward, guidance and the company of those whom Allah has favored: “and then We would certainly give them from Us a great reward,” (Quran 4:67) “and would surely guide them to a straight path.” (Quran 4:68) “Those who obey Allah and the Messenger will be with those whom Allah has blessed: the prophets, the people of Truth, the martyrs, and the righteous[1]. What excellent companions they are!” (Quran 4:69) “That grace is from Allah, and sufficient is Allah as Knower.” (Quran 4:70) There is also the good life promised to a believer who acts righteously: “Whoever does righteous deeds, male or female, while being a believer, We will surely grant him a good life[1], and We will surely reward them according to the best of their deeds[2].” (Quran 16:97)

Supplication, mindfulness of Allah and striving against harmful thoughts therefore work together to safeguard the heart, while the believer remains certain that the reins of hearts and the scale of raising and lowering are in the Hand of the Most Merciful.
