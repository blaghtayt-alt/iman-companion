Knowing and Enumerating Allah’s Names

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Messenger of Allah, may Allah’s peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Allah has ninety-nine names, i.e. one-hundred minus one, and whoever knows them will go to Paradise.”
Bukhari 2736; Muslim 2677.
Knowing Allah through His names and attributes is the foundation of the heart’s life and faith. A person does not fulfill faith simply by saying, “I believe in Allah,” while turning away from knowing the One in whom he believes. Rather, he strives to understand the perfection and majesty through which Allah has made Himself known, so that his certainty grows.
This is the noblest field of knowledge because of the nobility of its subject. Studying it is part of what the servant was created for: knowing and worshipping his Lord. How unworthy it is for someone who continually receives blessings to remain ignorant of the One who bestows them and turn away from Him.
This knowledge bears fruit in love for Allah, reverent awe and fear of Him, hope in Him, and sincerity in working for Him. Knowing His names thus opens a way to reform the heart and remove the anxieties and ailments that afflict it.
A man appointed to command an expedition used to conclude his recitation in prayer with the chapter al-Ikhlas. When asked why, he said that it describes the Most Merciful and that he loved to recite it. The Prophet, may Allah’s peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Tell him that Allah loves him.” [Bukhari 7375; Muslim 813]. A separate Anas report concerning love for this chapter says: “Your love for it shall have you admitted into Paradise” [Tirmidhi 2901; compare the shorter Ahmad 12432 report]. The love in question concerns what the chapter makes known of Allah’s attributes; it is more than a habit of repeating its words.
Enumerating the names does not mean merely counting them aloud. It includes memorizing their wording, understanding their meanings and what they signify, believing in them, worshipping Allah as they require, and calling upon Him by them, both through worship and through asking Him for what one needs.
Accordingly, Ibn al-Qayyim arranged the levels of enumeration as memorizing the names, then understanding them, then calling upon Allah by them. Al-Saadi explained the promise of entry into Paradise in terms of memorizing them, understanding them, believing in them, and worshipping Allah through them.
Someone who memorizes the name “the All-Hearing” while letting his tongue say what Allah dislikes has not gained the fruit of knowing that name as someone does who watches over his speech and what he keeps hidden.
Each attribute has an effect on a person’s worship. Whoever knows that Allah alone governs harm and benefit, giving and withholding, creation and provision, life and death, grows stronger in reliance upon Him, and that reliance becomes evident in his actions. Whoever knows His hearing, sight and knowledge, and that He knows secrets and what is still more hidden, the treachery of the eyes and what hearts conceal, guards his tongue, limbs and passing thoughts, directing them toward what pleases Him. Whoever knows His freedom from need, generosity, goodness, mercy and abundant kindness grows stronger in hope, which bears fruit in outward and inward deeds. Whoever knows His perfection and beauty loves Him and longs to meet Him.
All forms of worship are therefore connected to knowing the names and attributes. As that knowledge grows, faith grows stronger and more steadfast.
Through it, the servant also understands that everything Allah tells us is true, His commands and prohibitions are just and wise, and His actions are characterized by justice, generosity and wisdom. He thus recognizes what His attributes entail in what He tells us, what He legislates and what He does.
Allah is to be known through what is given in the Quran and the Sunnah, affirming His names and attributes without distorting or denying them, likening them to those of creation, or specifying how they are. Pursuing approaches that strip the attributes of their meanings or make them like created attributes prevents the intended effect of this knowledge.
The connection between this knowledge and reverent fear is expressed in the statement cited from Fatir 35:28. The complete containing verse reads: just as humans, animals and livestock are of various colors. Only the people of knowledge among His slaves fear Allah. Indeed, Allah is All-Mighty, All-Forgiving. Someone who knows Allah’s power to do whatever He wills and the reality of recompense fears Him and avoids disobedience. The more complete one’s knowledge of the Magnificent One, whose attributes are perfect, the greater that reverent fear becomes.
Ahmad ibn Asim al-Antaki said: “Whoever has more knowledge of Allaah, will have more fear of Him.” (English translation by Abu Safwan Farid ibn Abdul’wahid Haibatan, Causes Behind the Increase and Decrease of Eemaan, p. 52, note 70). Ibn al-Qayyim explained that the soul’s greatest need is to know its Creator, love Him, remember Him and rejoice in Him, and that increasing knowledge of His attributes brings it closer to Him.
The number ninety-nine does not restrict Allah’s names to that number. The report concerns the virtue of enumerating these names. It is like a man saying that he has set aside a thousand dirhams for charity: this does not mean that all his wealth amounts to only a thousand.
The report concerning anxiety and sorrow reads in the published English translation: Abdullah ibn Mas’ud reported: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said, “A servant is not struck by worry or grief but let him say, ‘O Allah, I am Your servant, the son of Your servant, the son of Your maidservant. My forelock is in Your hand, Your decision over me has passed, and Your decree upon me is in justice. I ask You by every name with which You have named Yourself, or revealed in Your Book, or taught to one of Your creatures, or kept hidden in the Unseen with You, that You make the Quran the spring of my heart, the light within me, the banisher of my depression, and the expeller of my anxiety,’ then Allah will expel his worry and replace his grief with joy.” It was said, “O Messenger of Allah, shall we not study these words?” The Prophet said, “Of course, whoever hears them should study them.” (Musnad Ahmad 3712; English translation by Abu Amina Elias, Justin Parrott.) If all the names were known and confined to a fixed total, the reference to what Allah has kept in His own knowledge would not carry this meaning.
The names by which Allah has named Himself fall into three categories in this supplication: those He has taught to whomever He wills of His creation without revealing them in His Book; those He has revealed in His Book, making Himself known to His servants through them; and those whose knowledge He has kept to Himself, disclosing them to no one. The words “with which You have named Yourself” embrace all three categories, rather than forming a fourth category.
What He has reserved to Himself here is knowledge of those names. This does not mean that the other names are not also names uniquely belonging to Allah.
The mention of the Greatest Name shows that the names differ in rank in a way Allah knows, while all of them are perfect.
The servant may appeal to Allah through His names generally, both those he knows and those he does not know. The passage cited from al-Araf 7:180 occurs in the following complete verse: Allah has the Most Beautiful Names, so call upon Him by them, and leave those who deviate concerning His Names[79]. They will be recompensed for what they used to do. Supplication is therefore not confined to the names one has memorized.
The wording of the supplication in distress reveals the connection between knowledge and the state of the heart. It begins by acknowledging that a person is Allah’s servant, the child of His male servant and His female servant; that his forelock is in his Lord’s hand; that His judgment takes effect upon him; and that His decree concerning him is just. It then appeals through His names for the Quran to become the springtime of the heart, the light of the breast and the clearing away of sorrow.
This knowledge is no intellectual luxury. It involves recognizing one’s need, worshipping Allah alone, thinking well of Him and acknowledging His justice: foundations on which a person relies in distress. It finds its nourishment in the Quran, which that person recites, reflects upon and puts into practice.
The most direct ways to increase this knowledge are to reflect on Allah’s words, consider His actions toward His allies and His enemies, and ponder the evidence of power, knowledge, mercy and justice that He has related. When a person reflects that Allah is Living and does not die, sustains all things and does not sleep, knows all things so that not even an atom’s weight escapes Him, hears all voices distinctly, whatever their languages and needs, sees the hidden details of His creatures, and directs the affairs of the world, his heart fills with reverence and awareness that Allah watches over him.
Ibn al-Qayyim explains that Allah’s names are most beautiful because they are names of praise, commendation and glorification. His attributes are perfection and majesty, and His actions are wisdom, mercy and justice. He did not create creation in vain or leave human beings without purpose. Rather, He made Himself known to them, varied His signs for them, showered them with blessings, called them to love Him and prescribed mercy for Himself.
The effect of enumerating the names therefore extends beyond a memorized list. It becomes a living knowledge present in fear, hope, love, reliance upon Allah and action.

[79] i.e., use them improperly such as calling other gods besides Allah by them, or deny them, or distort their meanings.

Knowing and enumerating the names

The words “knows them” in Bukhari 2736 correspond to the Arabic ihsa, the enumeration explained in this lesson through memorizing, understanding, belief, worship and calling upon Allah. Published explanation of the names report (Bukhari and Muslim): This Hadīth states that Allah, the Almighty, has ninety-nine names, and that whoever memorizes them all, believes in their meanings, and acts according to their implications, in matters other than those reserved exclusively for Allah, the Almighty, will enter Paradise. Making an oath by any of these names is permissible and binding. Consequently, one must offer expiation if he broke an oath sworn by one of the names of Allah, the Most Merciful and the Ever Merciful, or by one of His attributes such as His Face, Greatness, Grandeur, and Might. Ibn al-Qayyim’s three levels and al-Saadi’s explanation appear in Causes Behind the Increase and Decrease of Eemaan, translated by Haibatan, pp. 49–50. The cited works are Bada’i al-Fawa’id 1:164 and al-Saadi’s al-Tawdih wa-l-Bayan, p. 26.

The expedition commander

The commander’s account is from ‘Aishah (Bukhari 7375; Muslim 813); the published report is assessed as authentic. Published explanation: The Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him) appointed a man in charge of an army unit to be in charge and judge between them so that everything take place in an orderly fashion and without chaos. He chose the most religious, knowledgeable, and expert one of them. For this reason, rulers and scholars were to lead the prayers due to their knowledge and religion. He always concluded his recitation with Sūrat al-Ikhlās in the second Rak‘ah of every prayer because of his love of Allah and His names and attributes. It is natural whenever someone loves something that he increases its remembrance. When they returned to Madīnah, they reported this to the Prophet. So he said: "Ask him why he does this!" Is it a coincidence, or is there a special reason? The leader said: "I do this because this Sūrah contains the attributes of the Most Merciful and I love to repeat it because of this. So the Prophet (may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him) said: "Inform him, just as he repeats this Sūrah - because of what it contains of Allah's beautiful names and attributes and because he loves it - Allah loves him as well."

Separate Anas reports and their assessments

The shorter Anas report is Ahmad 12432; Shuayb al-Arnaut calls it sound. The longer account at the mosque of Quba is Tirmidhi 2901. Its edition record gives Ahmad Muhammad Shakir: Hasan Sahih; al-Albani: Hasan Sahih; Bashar Awad Maarouf: Hasan; and Zubair Ali Zai: “Sahih Bukhari”. These assessments belong to their respective reports. The teacher’s transcript cites the Tirmidhi account as 2903 and also refers to a suspended Bukhari version. Neither Anas account is ‘Aishah’s expedition-commander account.

Knowledge and fear

The saying is attributed to Ahmad ibn Asim al-Antaki. Haibatan’s translation of Causes Behind the Increase and Decrease of Eemaan, p. 52, note 70, identifies the speaker and cites al-Qushayri’s al-Risalah, p. 141, and his biography in Siyar 11:409. A separate Arabic witness is al-Marwazi, Ta‘zim Qadr al-Salah, report 786. Ibn al-Qayyim’s teaching that follows is a separate explanation.

The supplication for distress

Al-Albani authenticated the report in al-Silsilah al-Sahihah 199, as cited by the teacher and by Abu Amina Elias. Shuayb al-Arnaut’s assessment of the particular Ahmad 3712 chain is weak. These are separate attributed judgments. The published English prayer places revelation in the Book before teaching a creature. The Arabic report and the lesson’s Arabic text place teaching before revelation; the later explanation follows that Arabic order. The article’s Arabic outcome reads farajan (relief), while the teacher’s Arabic reads farahan (joy). These are distinct witnesses. The quoted English prayer, outcome and reply about learning remain the wording of the one published translation.
