When Revelation Came: From Hira to Khadijah

Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, grew up in Makkah within the society described in the previous chapter, but he was not removed from the ordinary responsibilities of life. One of the jobs he is authentically known to have done before prophethood was shepherding sheep. In a hadith narrated by Abu Hurayrah, the Prophet explained that every prophet sent by Allah had tended sheep. When his Companions asked whether he had done so too, he said that he had shepherded sheep for the people of Makkah for a small payment called qirats.

Allah did not send a prophet except that he tended sheep.

Sahih al-Bukhari 2262 — HadeethEnc 6219

Shepherding may sound like simple work, but it demands patience and constant attention: the shepherd gathers what strays, protects the weak, and watches the flock so that no animal is lost or harmed. Scholars have therefore noted how this work trains a person in patience, gentleness, and responsibility. This was not unique to Muhammad; the hadith tells us that earlier prophets also passed through this experience.

The years passed, and Muhammad’s life in Makkah became settled. Before prophethood he had married Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, may Allah be pleased with her. Seerah sources describe Khadijah as a respected woman of Quraysh who possessed wealth and conducted trade, and who knew Muhammad’s honesty, trustworthiness, and good character before he became a prophet. She became his wife and his companion in family life. So when the greatest event of his life occurred years later, the first home he returned to was Khadijah’s.

When Muhammad reached the age of forty, the moment drew near when his life would change from that of a man well known in Makkah to that of a prophet carrying a message to humanity. The change did not arrive without any preparation. Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, reported that the beginning of revelation (Allah communicating guidance and religious rulings to His prophet) came through truthful dreams. He would see a dream and it would later occur as clearly as the light of dawn.

Then he came to love seclusion (spending time away from people). He began going to the Cave of Hira, a small cave high on a mountain northeast of Makkah, today known as Jabal al-Nur. There he would spend days and nights away from Makkah’s distractions in worship. He took provisions with him, and when they ran out he returned to Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, collected more provisions, and went back again.

This retreat was not a permanent abandonment of society. Muhammad still lived in Makkah and returned to his family, but Hira gave him a place away from the idols and the noise of the life around him.

Then came the night when everything changed.

Muhammad was in the Cave of Hira when Gabriel (Jibril), peace be upon him—the angel who brings Allah’s revelation to His prophets—came to him. Muhammad did not know beforehand that this night would mark the beginning of the Qur’an’s revelation.

The angel told him, ‘Read.’ Muhammad answered that he was not a reader. Gabriel embraced and pressed him firmly until he could bear no more, then released him and told him again to read. The exchange was repeated three times. Then Gabriel recited the first revealed verses:

Read in the name of your Lord Who created, created man from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, Who taught by the pen, taught man what he did not know.

Qur’an 96:1–5 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

These were the first verses of the Qur’an to be revealed. The message opened with the command to read, connected reading to the name of the Lord who creates, and spoke of human creation, teaching, the pen, and the knowledge Allah gives people that they did not previously possess.

After this encounter Muhammad returned from Hira shaken by what he had seen and heard. He did not go to the leaders of Makkah or to a public gathering. He went straight home to Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her.

He entered trembling and asked to be covered with garments. They covered him until his fear subsided. Then he told Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, what had happened and said that he feared for himself.

Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, could have become overwhelmed by fear herself, but she knew the man who had lived with her and the character he had shown for years. She reassured him:

No, by Allah, Allah will never fail you. Verily, you maintain kinship ties, help those in need, support the destitute, provide the guests with food and shelter, and assist those affected by calamities.

Sahih al-Bukhari 3 — excerpt from the English translation published by HadeethEnc, entry 66303.

Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, based her reassurance on the character she had known in her husband for years: he cared for relatives, supported those unable to manage on their own, helped the needy, honored guests and stood by people in hardship. These qualities gave her confidence that Allah would not abandon him to disgrace. She then sought advice from someone familiar with the earlier scriptures and accounts of revelation.

So she took him to Waraqah ibn Nawfal, one of her relatives. Waraqah had become a Christian before Islam, had knowledge of the scriptures of the People of the Book, and by then was an elderly blind man. Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, asked him to listen to Muhammad’s account.

Waraqah asked Muhammad what he had seen, and Muhammad described what had happened in the Cave of Hira. From what he knew of earlier prophets, Waraqah recognized the nature of the event and said that this was the same bearer of revelation whom Allah had sent to Moses.

This is the same Nāmūs (i.e., the angel) whom Allah had sent to Moses.

Sahih al-Bukhari 3 — excerpt from the English translation published by HadeethEnc, entry 66303.

The word he used, namus, meant the angel who brought revelation—in other words, Gabriel. Waraqah then wished that he could be young and strong during Muhammad’s mission, and that he might still be alive when Muhammad’s people drove him out of Makkah.

Muhammad was surprised and asked whether his own people would really expel him. Until then, he had lived among them for forty years and no open conflict had yet begun.

Waraqah answered that anyone who brought a message like this had faced hostility, and he promised that if he lived to see that day he would support Muhammad strongly.

Waraqah died not long after this meeting. Then there was a pause in revelation (a period during which no new Qur’anic revelation came after the first encounter). What followed would open the next stage: this was no longer only an extraordinary experience in a cave, but the beginning of the life of prophethood itself.

Makkah looked the same that night as it had before: the Ka‘bah stood in its place, the markets continued, and the tribes moved along their familiar routes. Yet in a small cave above the city, the first words of the Qur’an had been revealed, and Muhammad returned home carrying the beginning of a message that would transform his life and the lives of those around him.
