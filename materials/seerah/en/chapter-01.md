Chapter 1

A Protected House and a Promise Drawing Near

Long before Muhammad ﷺ was born, there were signs that preceded his coming. Not everyone knew about them, and people did not yet understand how their separate threads would one day come together as part of a single story.

In Makkah, beside the Ka‘bah, the House whose foundations Abraham and Ishmael, peace be upon them, had raised, Abraham, peace be upon him, prayed to his Lord with a supplication that would remain preserved in the Qur’an:

Our Lord, send among them a messenger to recite to them Your revelations, teach them the Book and wisdom, and purify them. You are the All-Mighty, the All-Wise.

Qur’an 2:129 — Published translation: Rowwad Translation Center

Abraham, peace be upon him, was asking Allah to raise from among the people of this land a messenger of their own: one who would recite Allah’s revelations to them, teach them, and purify them. Centuries passed and prophets came and went, until Jesus, son of Mary, peace be upon him, gave the Children of Israel glad tidings of a Messenger who would come after him:

And [remember] when Jesus, son of Mary, said, “O Children of Israel, I am truly a messenger of Allah to you, confirming the Torah which came before me, and giving glad tidings of a messenger after me whose name will be Ahmad.” But when he came to them with clear proofs, they said, “This is clear magic.”

Qur’an 61:6 — Published translation: Rowwad Translation Center

When asked about himself, the Prophet ﷺ linked his coming to Abraham’s prayer and the glad tidings given by Jesus, peace be upon them:

“I am the answer to the prayer of my father Ibraaheem, and the glad tidings of ‘Eesa…”

Hadith excerpt — English translation: Islam Q&A, answer 26713, quoting Ibn Hisham, 1/166. Parallel reports: Sahih Ibn Hibban 6404; Musnad Ahmad 17163.

The message that would later appear in Makkah, then, did not arrive without any earlier indication. Within the legacy of the prophets was news of a Messenger who would come near the end of time, and some knowledge of this remained among the People of the Book—the Jews and Christians.

That knowledge did not remain entirely among them. Some Jews spoke openly before the Arabs about the approach of a prophet. This was especially known in Yathrib, later known as Madinah, where Arab tribes and Jewish communities lived alongside one another.

When disputes arose between them and the Arab polytheists, some Jews would say that the time of a prophet was approaching and that, when he appeared, they would follow him and fight alongside him against them. The Qur’an later referred to this:

Although they used to pray for victory ˹by means of the Prophet˺ over the polytheists, when there came to them a Book from Allah which they recognized, confirming the Scripture they had ˹in their hands˺, they rejected it. So may Allah’s condemnation be upon the disbelievers.

Qur’an 2:89 — Dr. Mustafa Khattab, The Clear Quran, published on Quran.com.

The Ansar were the Muslims of Madinah who supported the Prophet ﷺ and the emigrants from Makkah. Some of them later reported that, before accepting Islam, they had heard statements like these from their Jewish neighbors. Then, when Muhammad ﷺ was finally sent, some of the people of Yathrib remembered what they had been hearing and recognized that this was the prophet whose coming had been spoken about.

But this was still knowledge circulating within a relatively limited circle: among the People of the Book and among Arabs who had lived close enough to them to hear it. Shortly before the birth of the Prophet ﷺ, however, something very different happened. This was not information known only to a few individuals. It was a major public event that drew the attention of the Arabs toward Makkah and toward the Ka‘bah itself.

Makkah was a town whose life centered around the Sacred House. Quraysh knew the position the Ka‘bah held among the Arabs, but they did not possess an army capable of confronting a great military force if one marched against them.

In Yemen was Abraha. He had built a magnificent church and wanted to draw the Arabs toward it. The books of Seerah report that a series of events eventually led him to march toward Makkah with an army, intending to destroy the Ka‘bah. An elephant was among that army, and so the event would remain known in Arab memory as the Event of the Elephant.

For the people of Makkah, the sight was terrifying. An army was approaching their city, and they had no force capable of stopping it. The books of Seerah report that Abraha’s men seized property belonging to the people of Makkah as they advanced. Among what they took were two hundred camels belonging to ‘Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim, the leader of Quraysh and the grandfather of the Prophet ﷺ.

When ‘Abd al-Muttalib entered before Abraha, Abraha was impressed by his appearance and dignity. But when he asked what he wanted, ‘Abd al-Muttalib asked for his camels to be returned. Abraha was surprised: he had come to destroy the House revered by Quraysh, yet the man representing Quraysh before him was asking about his camels.

The books of Seerah famously attribute to ‘Abd al-Muttalib the words: “I am the owner of the camels and the Ka'bah has its Lord who will protect it.” These details are preserved in historical and Seerah reports and do not all carry the same level of certainty as the Qur’an or an authentic hadith. But the central event itself, and its outcome, were preserved in the Qur’an.
— IslamWeb, “Event of the Elephant,” fatwa 85617 (23 February 2003).

The army continued toward the Sacred House, but it never accomplished what it had come to do. Reports state that when the elephant was directed toward the Ka‘bah, it refused to advance, while if it was turned toward another direction, it would move. Then came the event the Arabs would continue to remember: Allah sent against the army flocks of birds pelting them with stones of baked clay. Their plan collapsed, and the force that had come to destroy the House was ruined.

1. Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the people of the Elephant?
2. Did He not turn their scheme into a total loss?
3. He sent against them swarms of birds,
4. pelting them with stones of baked clay,
5. leaving them like chewed-up chaff.

Qur’an 105:1–5 — Published translation: Rowwad Translation Center

The Qur’an did not introduce the event to the people of Makkah as though it were completely unknown. It asked, “Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the people of the Elephant?” It was speaking to them about an event that had become established in their collective memory. Its effect also went beyond the destruction of one army: the House remained standing, and the Arabs saw that a force Quraysh themselves could not have defeated had been prevented from reaching it. The sanctity of Makkah and the Sacred House became even greater in their eyes.

Some details of the Event of the Elephant were still known during the lifetime of the Prophet ﷺ. This becomes clear many years later at Hudaybiyah, a place near Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ and his Companions were traveling toward Makkah when his she-camel, al-Qaswa’, stopped. Some of the Companions said that al-Qaswa’ had become stubborn. The Prophet ﷺ explained that this was not her normal behavior and said:

“Al-Qaswa’ has not become stubborn, for stubbornness is not her habit, but she was stopped by Him Who stopped the elephant.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 2731–2732 — excerpt from the English translation published by Sunnah.com.

By referring to Allah as the One who stopped the elephant, the Prophet ﷺ recalled an event his listeners already knew: Allah had prevented the elephant from advancing toward the Sacred House. This hadith thus connects directly to the Event of the Elephant and preserves, through an authentic report, a detail of that event before the Prophet’s birth.

In the same year that later became known as the Year of the Elephant, Muhammad ibn ‘Abdullah ﷺ was born, according to the well-known view in the books of Seerah. ‘Abd al-Muttalib, who had stood as the leading figure of Quraysh during the confrontation with Abraha, was the grandfather of the child born that year. His father, ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib, had died — according to the most widely reported account — before his birth, so Muhammad ﷺ entered the world without his father.

Looking back on the Event of the Elephant after the rest of the Seerah became known, a number of scholars understood it as part of the preparation for what was to come. The House around which the mission of the final Prophet would later begin had been protected, its sanctity had grown in Arab hearts, and Makkah and Quraysh had gained a position of security and respect. This does not mean that the Arabs at the time knew that a prophet was about to be born; it is the place the event takes when the Seerah is read after the larger picture has become clear.

That security mattered for more than simply remaining safe inside Makkah. Makkah was not fertile agricultural land, and its people depended on food and goods coming from beyond the valley. Quraysh became known for two great trading journeys: the journey of winter and the journey of summer. Their merchants traveled into surrounding lands and returned with food, clothing, and other goods needed by the people of Makkah.

The security associated with the Sacred House was an important part of their ability to travel. Makkah had its sanctity, Quraysh were recognized as the people of the House, and Arab tribes knew their position. They could therefore travel along routes that might have been dangerous for others, while trade also came into Makkah, especially during pilgrimage seasons and the markets held around them.

1. For the accustomed security of the Quraysh,
2. secure in their winter and summer journeys.
3. Let them worship the Lord of this [Sacred] House,
4. Who fed them against hunger and made them secure against fear.

Qur’an 106:1–4 — Published translation: Rowwad Translation Center

Two blessings touched the daily life of Quraysh directly: food in a land whose own agriculture could not provide enough for its people, and security in an environment where tribes could otherwise live under the threat of raids and fear. But this part of the story does not begin with Surah Quraysh. To see where it began, we have to return hundreds of years earlier, once again to Abraham, peace be upon him, and to the prayer he made for this land:

And [remember] when Abraham said, “My Lord, make this city [of Makkah] a sanctuary and provide its people with fruits – those among them who believe in Allah and the Last Day.” [Allah] said, “As for those who disbelieve, I will grant them enjoyment for a short while, then I will force them into the punishment of the Fire. What a terrible destination!”

Qur’an 2:126 — Published translation: Rowwad Translation Center

Abraham, peace be upon him, had prayed for security, and he had prayed for provision. Centuries later, the Qur’an reminded the people of Makkah that the Lord of this House was the One who fed them against hunger and made them secure against fear. Here, the end of this part of the story returns to its beginning: Abraham, peace be upon him, raises the Sacred House, prays for Makkah to receive security and provision, and asks Allah to send among its people a messenger from among themselves. Centuries pass. Jesus, peace be upon him, gives glad tidings of a Messenger to come after him. Knowledge of the expected prophet remains among the People of the Book, and some of it reaches the Arabs. Then an army marches toward the Ka‘bah intending to destroy it, but it is prevented from reaching the House. The Ka‘bah remains standing, Quraysh live with a security that supports their trading journeys, and in that same city, in the Year of the Elephant, Muhammad ibn ‘Abdullah ﷺ is born.

The people of Makkah did not know then what would begin some forty years later. But when these events are read after the Seerah has unfolded, their separate threads can be seen together: a House beside which Abraham, peace be upon him, prayed; a promise preserved in the legacy of the prophets; an event remembered throughout Arabia; and security and provision surrounding the people of the Sacred House. Here begins the story of the child born in Makkah whose father had died before his birth: Muhammad ibn ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib ﷺ.
