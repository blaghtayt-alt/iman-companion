Chapter Nine

Proclaim What You Are Commanded: From the Years of Concealment to the Call from al-Safa

News of Islam had begun to spread in Makkah. People were talking about Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, and about those who had believed in him, while many details of the Muslims' movements and the identities of some believers still remained concealed. Then a new stage began: the call was no longer to remain limited to those whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, could reach privately.

Allah says:

“Then proclaim what you are commanded, and turn away from those who associate partners with Allah.” — Qur’an 15:94 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

And He says:

“And warn [starting with] the nearest kinsfolk,” — Qur’an 26:214 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

[Ibn Hisham explained the Arabic expression “فَاصْدَعْ” (fasda‘) as meaning to separate and openly declare what one has been commanded, so that truth becomes distinct from falsehood. Al-Suhayli expanded on the image by comparing the emergence of truth to dawn breaking through the darkness of night.] The central meaning is clear: the message would no longer be conveyed in exactly the same way as at the beginning. The call was entering a stage of open proclamation and direct confrontation.

What Was Happening Before This Proclamation?

The Seerah sources commonly give about three years for the period of concealment. Ibn Ishaq gives this duration while qualifying it as information that had reached him. No authentic hadith fixes the length of this period decisively. The three years are therefore the duration commonly reported in the Seerah sources, rather than a period established by an undisputed text.

What matters is that those years were not years of silence.

After the first verses of Surat al-'Alaq were revealed, the first pause in revelation occurred. Then Allah revealed:

“O you covered [in your cloak], arise and warn, and proclaim the greatness of your Lord, and purify your garments, and shun idol worshiping,” — Qur’an 74:1–5 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

It is authentically reported from Jabir ibn ‘Abdullah, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke about the pause in revelation, then mentioned seeing the angel who had come to him at Hira and the revelation of the opening verses of Surat al-Muddaththir. The account continues:

“After that, the revelation became intense and continuous.”

— Sahih al-Bukhari 4, 4926. Excerpt from the published translation of no. 4: HadeethEnc, 66304.

Thus, the same years in which the Muslims concealed many aspects of their affairs were also years in which the Qur'an continued to descend, the Prophet received it, and the believers learned it from him.

For that reason, the expression "the secret call" needs some precision. It did not mean that Makkah knew nothing about Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, the Qur'an, or the existence of people who followed him. What was often concealed were the identities of individuals, meetings, and certain details of worship and movement. The basic news of the message itself was gradually reaching people.

This is illustrated by the report of ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, concerning Allah's words:

“[…] Do not be too loud in your prayer, nor too quiet, but seek a way in between.” — Qur’an 17:110 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

Ibn ‘Abbas said that this verse was revealed while the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was keeping himself concealed in Makkah. When he prayed with his Companions, he would recite the Qur'an aloud. When the polytheists heard him, they insulted the Qur'an, the One who revealed it, and the one who brought it. He was therefore instructed not to raise his voice so much that they would hear and abuse the Qur'an, and not to lower it so much that his Companions could not hear him.

— Sahih al-Bukhari 4722.

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was concealed in Makkah, yet the polytheists sometimes heard his recitation, knew something of his message, and objected to it. This makes the nature of the concealment clearer: it was not complete absence of public awareness, but a stage in which the spread of the message was managed cautiously.

The Qur'an That Was Being Revealed During Those Years

As for which surahs were revealed during that period, we cannot produce a complete list with a certain chronological order: first this surah, then that one, then the next, and so on.

We know with certainty that the opening of Surat al-'Alaq was the first revelation and that the opening verses of Surat al-Muddaththir came after the pause in revelation. Beyond that, we do not possess a comprehensive authentic report from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions that would allow us to construct a complete sequence of every surah without uncertainty.

There are reports from some leading Tabi‘un—the generation of Muslims who met the Companions—about particular surahs. These reports can serve as indications, but do not establish a definitive chronological order for the whole Quran.

One of the surahs with strong signs of being very early is Surat al-Qalam. It is reported from Mujahid and ‘Ubayd ibn ‘Umayr that it was among the early revelations, although that does not place it before al-Muddaththir, whose early revelation is established through authentic hadith.

At the beginning of Surat al-Qalam, Allah says:

“Nūn. By the pen and what they [i.e., the angels] write [in the Records of deeds], By the grace of your Lord [O Prophet], you are not a madman.” — Qur’an 68:1–2 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

This preserves part of what was being said around the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Makkah. Accusations associated with madness, jinn, and devils had begun to appear, and the Qur'an reassured the Prophet and rejected that accusation.

In Surat al-Hijr itself, the surah in which the command “Then proclaim what you are commanded […]” appears, Allah also records the words of the deniers:

“They say, ‘O you to whom the Reminder has been sent down, You are surely insane! […]’” — Qur’an 15:6 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

This alone does not allow us to conclude that all of Surat al-Hijr was revealed at one time; a surah can be revealed in separate portions. But it does confirm that the Qur'an itself preserved something of the language with which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was confronted.

Surat al-A‘la is also among the surahs that may belong to the early period. It contains direct speech to the Prophet concerning the reception of the Qur'an:

“We will teach you [the Qur’an], so you will not forget, except what Allah wills, for indeed He knows what is manifest and what is hidden.” — Qur’an 87:6–7 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

We cannot establish its exact place in the chronology of revelation. We do know, however, that it was known to Muslims well before the Prophet's migration to Madinah. Al-Bara' ibn ‘Azib, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum, may Allah be pleased with them, were among the first people to reach Madinah and teach its people the Qur'an. Al-Bara' said:

“He (the Prophet (ﷺ) ) did not come (to Medina) till I had learnt Surat Al-Ala and also other similar Suras.”

— Sahih al-Bukhari 4941. Excerpt from the published English translation on Sunnah.com.

Thus, during the earliest Makkan years, the Qur'an was building the believers' knowledge of Allah and the Last Day, strengthening the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, teaching him how to receive revelation, and at the same time answering what the deniers had begun to say.

Listeners the People of Makkah Could Not See

The reports of that period include another incident that shows the effect produced by the revelation of the Qur'an. Scholars have discussed its precise date, however, so it should not be placed at a fixed point in the chronology with certainty.

Ibn ‘Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, reported that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, set out with a group of his Companions toward the market of ‘Ukaz. At that time, the devils had been prevented from obtaining news from heaven, and shooting stars were sent against them.

Groups of jinn returned to one another saying that something must have happened, and they set out east and west searching for the cause of the change.

One group headed toward Tihamah and passed by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, at Nakhlah, a valley on the route between Makkah and Ta'if, while the Messenger of Allah was praying with his Companions.

When they heard the Qur'an, they stopped to listen. Then they said to one another that this was what had come between them and the news of heaven.

They returned to their people saying:

“[…] Indeed, we have heard a wondrous recitation that guides to the right way, so we have believed in it, and we will never associate anyone with our Lord.” — Qur’an 72:1–2 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

Allah then informed His Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about them:

“Say, ‘It has been revealed to me that a group of jinn listened [to the Qur’an,] […]’”

Qur’an 72:1 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

— Sahih al-Bukhari 4921.

Scholars differed over the dating of this incident, and some considered an early period of the mission more likely. It is therefore mentioned here as an event that may have occurred early, not as a fixed chronological point before the public proclamation.

At a time when many people of Makkah were still disputing the Qur'an or accusing its bearer, listeners from a world invisible to human beings heard it and returned to their own people as warners.

Then Came the Command to Go Out to the People

After those years, the mission entered a new stage.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was no longer only to call those whom he could reach privately, nor were the believers simply to meet away from the sight of most people.

Allah says:

“And warn [starting with] the nearest kinsfolk,” — Qur’an 26:214 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, went to al-Safa, an elevated place close to the Ka‘bah, and climbed it.

From al-Safa he began calling the major tribal branches of Quraysh:

“O Bani Fihr! O Bani `Adi!”

until the people gathered.

Those who could not come themselves sent someone to find out what had happened. Abu Lahab was among those who came. His name was ‘Abd al-‘Uzza ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib, and he was an uncle of the Messenger of Allah.

When they had gathered, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked whether they would believe him if he told them that enemy cavalry in the valley intended to attack them.

They replied:

“Yes, for we have not found you telling anything other than the truth.”

They had known Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, before his mission and knew that he was not someone who lied to them. After receiving that testimony from them concerning the life they knew him to have lived among them, he told them why he had gathered them:

“I am a plain warner to you of a coming severe punishment.”

— Sahih al-Bukhari 4770 and 4971. Published excerpts: Muhammad Muhsin Khan, Sunnah.com; the warning follows the parallel wording in 4971.

The call was not a long speech detailing legal rulings. It was a warning. He had gathered them to tell them plainly that what he was warning them against was more serious than an army that might suddenly descend on Makkah from beyond the mountain.

Another authentic report shows how fully this warning included even those closest to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him.

He addressed both his tribe and his closest relatives:

He urged Quraysh to save themselves, explaining that he could not save them from Allah’s punishment. He gave the same warning to Banu ‘Abd Manaf, to his uncle al-‘Abbas ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib, to his aunt Safiyyah, and to his daughter Fatimah. He told Fatimah: “Ask what you wish from my property, but I cannot save you from Allah (if you disobey Him).”

— Sahih al-Bukhari 4771. Indirect account followed by an excerpt from the published translation by Muhammad Muhsin Khan, Sunnah.com.

Banu ‘Abd Manaf was a major branch of Quraysh and included households such as Banu Hashim, the Prophet's own family.

Kinship therefore did not create an exception to the message.

His uncle al-‘Abbas, his aunt Safiyyah, his daughter Fatimah, may Allah be pleased with them, and Quraysh as a whole were all addressed by the warning. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, could not make lineage alone a means of salvation before Allah.

The First Public Response

In that gathering, not everyone simply listened to the warning and then left.

Abu Lahab wished ruin on him for the rest of that day and protested:

“You gathered us only for this reason?” — Sahih al-Bukhari 4971; published translation by Muhammad Muhsin Khan, Sunnah.com. The fuller account is in 4770.

Then Allah revealed:

“May the hands of Abu Lahab perish, and may he perish! Neither his wealth nor his worldly gains will avail him.” — Qur’an 111:1–2 — Rowwad Translation Center, IslamHouse/QuranEnc.

Thus, one of the earliest public responses that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, received from his own relatives was the open rejection of his own uncle in front of the people.

But after that, the call could no longer return to the position it had occupied before.

The tribal branches of Quraysh had heard the warning together.

They understood that Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, was not presenting himself as a man who had simply chosen a private form of worship for himself, as some people before Islam had abandoned idol worship on their own. He was saying that Allah had sent him to them, that worshipping anything other than Allah was false, and that resurrection, judgment, and punishment lay ahead if people persisted in denial.

From this point, Quraysh would begin trying other means to stop the call.

One of the first paths they would take would be to go to Abu Talib, the Prophet's uncle and the chief of Banu Hashim, and ask him to restrain his nephew from them.
